🔑 *Answers — Daily Quiz* | 7 NOV 2026
1-(b) · 2-(d) · 3-(c) · 4-(a) · 5-(d) · 6-(a) · 7-(c) · 8-(a) · 9-(c) · 10-(d)

📖 Explanations:
1. (b) Anandamath 1882 → Tagore sang it 1896 → Swadeshi slogan 1905 → equal status 24 Jan 1950. Trap: the song was written in 1875 but Anandamath came only in 1882, so Tagore's 1896 rendition is after it.
2. (d) (d) is incorrect: the 1971 Act covers the National Anthem; the 2026 Amendment Bill proposes to add the National Song. (a), (b) and (c) are correct.
3. (c) II is wrongly matched: 1929 was his knighthood; the Nobel Prize came in 1930. The other three pairs are correct.
4. (a) All three are correct, so the student is wrong on both counts: the Nobel work was in Calcutta (affiliation Calcutta University), IISc came in 1933, and 28 Feb is the discovery date (birthday 7 Nov).
5. (d) II is wrong (reversed): 1903 = Physics (shared with Pierre Curie and Becquerel), 1911 = Chemistry (radium and polonium). I, III and IV are correct.
6. (a) II is wrong: lithium plants are at least 30 kt; 50 kt is the minimum for nickel plants. I, III and IV are correct.
7. (c) (c): selection is two-stage — technical criteria, then financial bidding where the lowest support sought wins. It is a capital subsidy (30%), not a PLI; capacity alone or State nomination does not decide.
8. (a) Tiger Triumph = India–US HADR (Visakhapatnam); Malabar = four-nation naval; Rahat Mitra = with Myanmar; Pralay = RCI Hyderabad missile. Trap in (b): swapping Tiger Triumph and Malabar.
9. (c) I is wrong: Anuradha Bhasin protected speech and trade through the internet and required proportionality for shutdowns, but did not decide whether access itself is a fundamental right. II and III are correct.
10. (d) (d): the Court called blanket bans unproven and universal age verification an overreach, and preferred safety-by-design. (a) is wrong — the right covers children; (b) — the basis is Art 21 + 19(1)(a); (c) — the Court gave no under-16 ban (that is Australia's law).
