🗓️ Ambedkar's last speech in the Constituent Assembly — the 'Grammar of Anarchy'
On 25 November 1949, one day before the Constitution was adopted, Dr B.R. Ambedkar warned India of three dangers — words still quoted in every debate on democracy.
⚠️ Exam trap: The speech was on 25 Nov 1949 — the Constitution was adopted on 26 Nov 1949 and came into force on 26 Jan 1950. The hero-worship warning is credited to J.S. Mill; 'gratitude cannot cost liberty' is Daniel O'Connell; 'a House divided' is Lincoln. 'Grammar of Anarchy' includes satyagraha — not only violent methods.

📰 Anti-defection law: Supreme Court seeks replies on a plea against 'strategic resignations'
A PIL says legislators escape disqualification by resigning while the Speaker is still deciding. The Court has asked the Centre, the Election Commission and the Law Commission to respond.
⚠️ Exam trap: The 'split' (one-third) exemption no longer exists — only the two-thirds merger remains. 1985 is the law (52nd Amendment); 1992 is the Kihoto Hollohan judgment. Tenth Schedule disqualification is decided by the Speaker/Chairman, not by the Election Commission.

📰 West Bengal to propose 'Paschim Banga': how a State's name is changed
The proposal will be tabled in the Assembly on 9 December. Only Parliament can finally change the name — the same Article 3 route used for Kerala → Keralam in August 2026.
⚠️ Exam trap: A State Assembly can only propose; Parliament decides. The State's views are not binding. A renaming law changes Schedules but is NOT an amendment under Article 368. Kerala's Assembly resolution was in 2024; Parliament passed the Bill in 2026.
