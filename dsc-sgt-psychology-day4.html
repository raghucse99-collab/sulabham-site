Topic 7: Memory, Forgetting and Motivation

1. Basic idea

Memory (జ్ఞాపకశక్తి / స్మృతి) is the ability to take in, keep and bring back what we learn. Forgetting (విస్మృతి) is the failure to bring it back. Motivation (ప్రేరణ) is the inner force that starts, directs and keeps up behaviour towards a goal. A child who forgets everything, or who doesn't want to learn, learns little, however good the teaching.

2. Why it exists

Exam success depends on remembering, and classroom success depends on wanting to learn. Knowing how memory works tells the teacher when to revise. Knowing what motivates children tells the teacher how to make them want to learn.

3. How it works

Three stages of memory: encoding (taking in) → storage (keeping) → retrieval (bringing out).

Atkinson–Shiffrin model (three stores)

Store How long Capacity Example
Sensory memory Under 1 second (sight) to 2–4 seconds (sound) Large A flash of light you just saw
Short-term memory (STM) About 20–30 seconds without rehearsal 7 ± 2 items (George Miller) Holding an OTP in mind
Long-term memory (LTM) Years, lifelong Practically unlimited Your mother tongue

Types of memory to know: rote memory (mechanical repetition without meaning) vs logical memory (based on understanding; for example, writing answers in your own words, the 2025 answer); recall (bringing out without help, like fill-in-the-blanks) vs recognition (identifying from options, like MCQs; this is easier).

Forgetting

  • Hermann Ebbinghaus used nonsense syllables (ZOK, DAX). His forgetting curve shows that most forgetting happens soon after learning, then it slows down.
  • Causes: decay (disuse); interference: proactive (old learning blocks new) and retroactive (new learning blocks old); repression (Freud: painful memories pushed out).
  • To remember better: spaced revision (especially the same day), understanding meaning, mnemonics (e.g. VIBGYOR), overlearning, enough sleep, linking new to old knowledge.

Motivation

  • Intrinsic (from inside: the story is interesting) vs extrinsic (from outside: marks, prizes, praise, fear). Intrinsic motivation lasts longer.
  • Motivation cycle: need → drive → incentive → goal (hunger → restlessness → food → eating).
  • Achievement motivation (David McClelland): the desire to succeed and do well. High achievers prefer moderately difficult goals that match their ability. Teachers develop it by setting goals according to each child's ability, the 2025 answer.
  • Level of aspiration: the goal a child sets for themselves. It should be neither too high (frustration) nor too low (no effort).

Abraham Maslow's hierarchy of needs

4. Everyday analogy

Memory is like a mobile phone. The screen is sensory memory: whatever flashes on it is gone in a moment unless you notice it. The notification bar is short-term memory: it holds a few items for a short time. The gallery is long-term memory: it stores a great deal, but you must save things there (encoding) and know where to find them (retrieval). Motivation is the battery charge: a fully loaded phone with no charge is useless.

5. Breaking the difficult parts

  1. Rote vs logical memory: repeating "7 × 8 = 56" fifty times (rote) vs understanding that 7 × 8 is seven eights (logical). Logical memory lasts longer and adapts to new questions.
  2. Proactive vs retroactive interference: "pro" = forward: the old blocks the new. "Retro" = backward: the new blocks the old.
  3. Recall vs recognition: an essay answer needs recall; a multiple-choice question needs only recognition.
  4. Why same-day revision works: the forgetting curve is steepest in the first hours, so a short revision then saves much more than a long one a week later.
  5. Intrinsic vs extrinsic in class: praise and prizes (extrinsic) can start interest; aim to move children towards curiosity and enjoyment (intrinsic).
  6. Deficiency vs growth needs (Maslow): levels 1–4 are deficiency needs; level 5 (self-actualisation) is a growth need. A hungry child cannot focus on learning, which is why mid-day meals matter.

6. Exceptions and confusing areas

  • 7 ± 2 is the STM limit, not the long-term one.
  • Ebbinghaus studied forgetting with nonsense syllables; Miller gave 7 ± 2.
  • Recognition is easier than recall.
  • Achievement motivation: very easy or impossible goals are chosen by children who fear failure, not by high achievers.
  • Maslow's lowest need is physiological, not safety.

7. Connections to other topics

  • Day 3: memory is the retention and recall phases of Gagné; Thorndike's law of exercise and disuse.
  • Topic 8: repression is also a defence mechanism.
  • Methodology: recapitulation, revision periods, spaced practice, use of mnemonics.
  • Perspectives: mid-day meals and Maslow's physiological needs.

8. Memory technique

Memory stages: "E-S-R" = "Every Student Remembers".

Interference: "PRO = Past ruins Present; RETRO = Recent ruins Remembered."

Maslow from the bottom: "P-S-L-E-S" → "Please Save Little Eggs Safely": Physiological, Safety, Love, Esteem, Self-actualisation.

Topic 8: Personality, Mental Health and Children with Special Needs

1. Basic idea

Personality (మూర్తిమత్వం) is the total pattern of a person's thinking, feeling and behaving that makes them unique. The word comes from the Latin "persona", the mask worn by actors. Mental health is the ability to face problems, adjust and live happily with others. Children with special needs (CWSN) are children who need extra support to learn: those with disabilities, learning disabilities, or giftedness. Inclusive education teaches them in regular classrooms.

2. Why it exists

Children in one class differ in temperament: shy or bold, calm or anxious. Some face stress at home; some have a disability or learning difficulty. A teacher who understands personality, adjustment and special needs can support every child instead of labelling some as "lazy" or "naughty".

3. How it works

Theories of personality

Type Given by Basis Types or traits
Type Hippocrates Body fluids Sanguine (cheerful), choleric (angry), melancholic (sad), phlegmatic (calm, slow)
Type Kretschmer Body build Pyknic (short, round), asthenic (thin), athletic
Type W.H. Sheldon Body build Endomorph (soft, relaxed), mesomorph (muscular, active), ectomorph (thin, sensitive)
Type Carl Jung Social nature Introvert, extrovert, ambivert
Type Eduard Spranger Values Theoretical, economic, aesthetic, social, political, religious
Trait G.W. Allport Traits Cardinal, central, secondary traits
Trait R.B. Cattell Traits 16 source traits; 16 PF questionnaire
Trait/type H.J. Eysenck Dimensions Introversion–extraversion, neuroticism–stability, psychoticism
Trait Big Five (OCEAN) Traits Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism
Psychoanalytic Sigmund Freud Structure of mind Id (pleasure), ego (reality), superego (conscience)

Factors of personality: biological (heredity, glands, physique, health), psychological (intelligence, emotions, self-concept) and social (family, school, peer group, culture, media).

Assessing personality

  • Subjective: interview, autobiography, case history.
  • Objective: questionnaires (16 PF), rating scales, observation, situational tests.
  • Projective: Rorschach Ink-Blot Test (10 cards); TAT (Murray and Morgan); CAT (Leopold Bellak, for ages 3–10); Word Association Test (Jung); sentence completion.

Mental health, adjustment and stress

  • Adjustment: balancing one's needs with the demands of the environment.
  • Conflicts (Kurt Lewin): approach–approach (two good choices), avoidance–avoidance (two bad choices), approach–avoidance (one choice with good and bad sides).
  • Stress: the body and mind's reaction to pressure. Symptoms: headache, irritability, poor sleep, low concentration, loss of appetite. Management: exercise, rest, talking to others, time planning, relaxation, realistic goals.
  • Emotional intelligence and managing emotions (Goleman, Day 2): recognise the feeling → pause → express it in a healthy way.

Defence mechanisms (Freud): unconscious ways the mind protects itself from anxiety.

Mechanism Meaning Example
Rationalisation A good-sounding excuse "The paper was out of syllabus."
Projection Putting your own faults on others A lazy student calls others lazy
Repression Pushing painful memories out of mind Forgetting a frightening accident
Denial Refusing to accept an unpleasant reality "I didn't fail; the results are wrong."
Displacement Venting anger on a safer target Scolded by father, the child beats his younger brother
Regression Going back to childish behaviour A 7-year-old sucks his thumb when a new baby arrives
Sublimation Turning energy into useful activity Channelling anger into sports
Compensation Making up for a weakness with a strength Weak in studies, excels in sports
Identification Copying someone admired A boy copies his cricket hero
Fantasy / day-dreaming Escaping into imagination Dreaming of being a topper instead of studying

Children with special needs and inclusive education

  • Inclusive education: all children, including CWSN, learn together in regular schools with support; the school changes to suit the child. Basis: RPwD Act 2016 (21 disabilities) and RTE 2009.
  • Learning disabilities: dyslexia (reading), dysgraphia (writing), dyscalculia (maths), dyspraxia (motor coordination). ADHD: inattention, over-activity, impulsiveness.
  • Gifted children (IQ above about 130): enrichment and challenge. Slow learners (IQ about 70–89): repetition, concrete examples and patience.
  • Hindrances to learning (asked in 2025): physical (poor health, sense defects), mental (low intelligence, poor attention), physical-environmental (poor light, noise, bad seating) and social-environmental (family conflict, poverty, peer problems).
  • Teacher's role: identify early, seat near the board, give simple instructions, use peer support and aids, teach remedially, and never label or mock.

4. Everyday analogy

Personality is like a house. The foundation is heredity, the walls are habits and traits, and the paint is the way we behave in public. Freud's id, ego and superego are like a child, a parent and a grandmother in the house. The child (id) wants sweets now, the grandmother (superego) says "that's wrong", and the parent (ego) finds a sensible middle way. Defence mechanisms are the curtains we pull so that others, and we ourselves, don't see the mess inside.

5. Breaking the difficult parts

  1. Type vs trait: a type puts people into boxes (introvert or extrovert); a trait measures how much of a quality each person has (a scale of sociability).
  2. Spranger is a type theorist (values); Eysenck works with dimensions. The 2025 paper grouped Hippocrates, Sheldon and Spranger as type theories, and Allport, Cattell and Eysenck as trait theories.
  3. Id, ego, superego: pleasure principle, reality principle, morality.
  4. Fantasy vs denial: fantasy = escaping into day-dreams; denial = refusing to accept reality. The 2025 paper's wrongly matched pair was "Fantasy – refusing unpleasant aspects".
  5. Inclusive vs integrated vs special: special = separate school; integrated = placed in a regular school with little change; inclusive = the school adapts.
  6. Mental vs social-environmental hindrance: low intelligence = mental; home problems = social-environmental.

6. Exceptions and confusing areas

  • Freud is not a type or trait theorist. In the 2025 paper, the "structure theories: Freud, Vygotsky, Erikson" option was wrong (Vygotsky is not a personality theorist).
  • Jung gave introvert/extrovert and the Word Association Test. Rorschach gave the ink-blot test.
  • TAT = Murray and Morgan; CAT = Bellak.
  • Rationalisation ≠ sublimation: an excuse vs useful redirection of energy.
  • RPwD 2016 = 21 disabilities; the old PwD Act 1995 had 7.

7. Connections to other topics

  • Day 1: Erikson's psychosocial stages are also a personality theory; adolescence and stress.
  • Day 2: emotional intelligence; intellectual disability and gifted children.
  • Perspectives: inclusive education, RPwD 2016, RTE.
  • Methodology: remedial teaching, individual attention, multi-level activities.

8. Memory technique

Hippocrates: "Sunny, Cross, Moody, Placid" → Sanguine (cheerful), Choleric (angry), Melancholic (sad), Phlegmatic (calm, slow).

Sheldon: "Endo = round belly (food), Meso = muscles, Ecto = thin edges".

Spranger's six values: "T-E-A-S-P-R" → "Teachers Earn Applause Serving People Respectfully" (theoretical, economic, aesthetic, social, political, religious).

Projective tests: "Ink–Rorschach, TAT–Murray, CAT–Bellak, Words–Jung."

Hindrances: "Body, Brain, Building, Background" = physical, mental, physical-environmental, social-environmental.
