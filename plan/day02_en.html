HISTORY · The Vedic Age: Early and Later Vedic society

Understand it first

What is it? After the Harappan cities faded, a new way of life grew in north India. Its people spoke an early form of Sanskrit and composed the Vedas. We call this the Vedic Age because our main source is the Vedic literature, not excavated cities. It has two parts: Early Vedic (Rigvedic) about 1500–1000 BCE (a tribal, cattle-herding society on the Sapta-Sindhu, the land of the seven rivers) and Later Vedic about 1000–600 BCE (settled farming kingdoms in the Ganga–Yamuna doab). Dates are approximate. Why it matters: the exam always asks the contrast between the two phases, and these ideas (varna, gotra, ashramas, kingship) shape later Indian history.

A manuscript page of the Rigveda, the oldest Veda (the Vedas were memorised and passed on orally for centuries before being written). (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, free licence)

2. Early Vedic (Rigvedic) society, c. 1500–1000 BCE

Place: the Sapta-Sindhu (land of seven rivers): the Indus, the five rivers of Punjab (Jhelum–Vitasta, Chenab–Asikni, Ravi–Parushni, Beas–Vipasa, Sutlej–Shutudri) and the Sarasvati, the most honoured river. The Ganga is hardly mentioned.

AspectEarly Vedic
PoliticsTribal rule. The unit was kula (family) → grama (village) → vis (clan) → jana (tribe). Tribes: Bharata, Purus, Yadus, Turvasas, Anus, Druhyus. Head: Rajan (chief, hereditary but checked by the tribe). Officers: Purohita (priest-adviser: Vashishtha, Vishvamitra), Senani (army), Gramani (village head). Sabha (council of elders) and Samiti (general assembly of the tribe) and Vidatha (oldest assembly; women also attended). No regular army, no regular tax: people gave voluntary gifts (bali). The king did not own land. Battle of the Ten Kings (Dasarajna) on the Parushni (Ravi): Sudas of the Bharata tribe won.
SocietyPatriarchal joint family (kula; father = kulapa). Women were respected: women poets such as Apala, Ghosha, Lopamudra, Vishvavara; they attended assemblies; no sati or child marriage. Three classes (varna = 'colour' or class) were mainly by work: Brahma (priests), Kshatriya (warriors), Vish/Vaishya (people); the fourth, Shudra, appears only in the late Purusha Sukta. Enemies were called Dasa/Dasyu.
EconomyPastoral: cattle were the main wealth ('gopati', 'gavishti' = search for cows = war). Farming was secondary (barley = yava). Horse and chariot were central. Metal: copper/bronze (ayas). Craftsmen: carpenters, weavers, smiths, potters. Barter; nishka was a gold ornament. No cities.
ReligionWorship of nature gods through hymns and fire sacrifice; no temples or idols. Indra (rain/thunder, 'Purandara' = fort-breaker; about 250 hymns, most), Agni (fire, messenger to gods; about 200 hymns), Varuna (guardian of Rita, cosmic order), Soma (sacred plant/drink), Surya, Ushas, Maruts, Ashvins. Male gods dominate.

3. Later Vedic society, c. 1000–600 BCE

Why things changed: people moved east to the Ganga–Yamuna doab and started to clear forests with iron tools (iron, called krishna/shyama ayas, is known from about 1000 BCE). Farming became the main work, a surplus grew, and with it came kings, priests and a stricter class order.

AspectLater Vedic
PoliticsTribes merged into larger territories, janapadas/rashtras: Kuru–Panchala (capital Hastinapur, then Kaushambi), Kosala, Kashi, Videha. The king's power grew through rituals: Rajasuya (coronation), Ashvamedha (horse sacrifice to claim supremacy), Vajapeya (chariot race). Titles: Samrat, Ekrat, Rajadhiraja. Advisers called Ratnins ('jewel-holders'). Regular tax: bali, bhaga. Vidatha vanished; Sabha and Samiti remained but women were excluded.
SocietyFour varnas became hereditary: Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra (lowest, no right to Vedic study). Priests and warriors were dominant. Gotra (clan from a common ancestor; marriage within the same gotra forbidden) appears. Ashramas: Brahmacharya, Grihastha, Vanaprastha (the fourth, Sannyasa, was not yet fixed). Women lost status; child marriage and dowry hints appear.
EconomyAgriculture became the main occupation: wheat, rice (vrihi), barley, pulses; iron ploughshare; PGW pottery. Crafts grew (metal-working, weaving, pottery, carpentry). Early towns (Hastinapur, Kaushambi) and trade increased; nishka and satamana were used as units of value.
ReligionIndra and Agni lost importance; Prajapati (creator), Vishnu (preserver) and Rudra (Shiva) rose. Sacrifices became long, costly and priest-controlled. The Upanishads questioned rituals and taught Atman–Brahman, karma, rebirth, moksha: the philosophy that Buddhism and Jainism soon challenged.
Example

Example to remember the shift: in the Early Vedic period a chief's wealth = number of cows, and war was 'search for cows'. In the Later Vedic period a king's wealth = fertile land and tax, so he needed priests to perform the Ashvamedha to prove his supremacy over many villages.

Exam trap

Gayatri Mantra: Rigveda Mandala 3. Purusha Sukta: Mandala 10. Oldest Veda: Rigveda (not Atharvaveda). Sabha, Samiti, Vidatha: Early Vedic (Vidatha disappears later). Gotra, four ashramas (three fixed), rigid varna, Rajasuya/Ashvamedha, iron, PGW: all Later Vedic. The king did not collect regular tax in the Early Vedic period. Indus (Sindhu) is the most mentioned river; Sarasvati the most sacred.

POLITY · Salient features of the Constitution

Understand it first

What is it? Day 1 told you how the Constitution was made. Now we ask: what is special in it, and where did its ideas come from? The Constituent Assembly did not copy one country; it borrowed the best working ideas from many constitutions and fitted them to Indian conditions, mainly following the Government of India Act 1935 for the administrative details. Knowing the features and their sources is a certain exam question.

Sources of the Indian Constitution

1. The main features, with the idea behind each

FeatureWhat it meansWhy / where it shows
Lengthiest written ConstitutionDetails of Centre, States, services, elections, emergency, all in one document (395 Articles originally; about 450 now).Because India is big and diverse, nothing was left to convention.
Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic RepublicWords of the Preamble: free from outside control; head of State is elected, not hereditary. (Socialist and Secular were added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976.)Studied fully on Day 3.
Parliamentary governmentReal power lies with the Council of Ministers, answerable to the Lok Sabha; the President is the nominal head.Taken from the UK; it gives close link between legislature and executive.
Federal with a unitary biasTwo levels of government but a strong Centre.Union List/State List/Concurrent List; Centre's emergency powers, Governor appointed by the Centre.
Fundamental Rights and Directive PrinciplesRights are enforceable in court; Directive Principles guide the State to build a welfare society (not enforceable).Together they are called the 'conscience' of the Constitution.
Independent and integrated judiciaryOne single pyramid: Supreme Court → High Courts → subordinate courts, which apply both Union and State laws (the USA has separate federal and State courts).Judges' security of tenure; judicial review.
Single citizenship, universal adult franchiseOne citizenship for all (no separate State citizenship). Every citizen aged 18+ can vote (lowered from 21 by the 61st Amendment 1988).A citizen of Andhra Pradesh who moves to Gujarat keeps the same citizenship and rights.
Emergency provisionsNational (Art 352), State (Art 356) and Financial (Art 360) emergencies; the Centre becomes stronger.From the Weimar Constitution of Germany.
Rigid and flexibleAmendment by Art 368: some parts by simple majority, most by special majority, and federal parts also need half of the States.Procedure from South Africa.
Fundamental Duties, Three-tier governmentDuties added by the 42nd Amendment (1976) on the Swaran Singh Committee (10 duties; the 11th added by the 86th Amendment 2002). 73rd and 74th Amendments (1992) added Panchayats and Municipalities.Duties idea from the USSR.

REASONING · Letter series and odd man out

Understand it first

What is it? Letter series is the number series of Day 1, but with letters. The trick: turn each letter into its position number (A = 1 to Z = 26), find the rule on the numbers, then turn back to letters. An odd man out question gives a group of items; all but one share a hidden property, and you must find the one that does not. Both appear every year.

ABCDEFGHIJKLM
12345678910111213
NOPQRSTUVWXYZ
14151617181920212223242526
Example

Memory tools: (1) EJOTY = positions 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 (multiples of 5). (2) Opposite letters: the letter's position from the left + position from the right = 27 (A–Z, B–Y, C–X, M–N). (3) Halfway: M = 13, N = 14.

1. Common letter-series patterns

PatternExampleAnswer and why
Constant skipB, E, H, K, N, ?Q: positions 2, 5, 8, 11, 14, so +3 → 17 = Q
Growing skipA, C, F, J, O, ?U: 1, 3, 6, 10, 15; gaps +2, +3, +4, +5, next +6 → 21 = U
Backward seriesZ, X, U, Q, L, ?F: 26, 24, 21, 17, 12; gaps −2, −3, −4, −5, next −6 → 6 = F
Pairs (front up, back down)AZ, BY, CX, DW, ?EV: first letter +1, second letter −1
Alternate two seriesA, Z, C, X, E, V, ?G: odd places A, C, E, G (+2); even places Z, X, V (−2)

2. Odd man out: how to attack

  • Letter groups: write positions, look at the gaps inside each group. Example: KMO (+2, +2), GIK (+2, +2), PRT (+2, +2), TVW (+2, +1): TVW is odd.
  • Pairs: check the sum or the difference of positions. Example: AZ (1+26 = 27), EV (5+22 = 27), IR (9+18 = 27), MN (13+14 = 27), QL (17+12 = 29): QL is odd.
  • Numbers: look for squares, cubes, primes, multiples. Example: 8, 27, 64, 100, 125 → all cubes except 100.
  • Words: look for category (animals, cities, rivers), but the exam often hides a second rule such as number of letters.
Exam trap

Always check both the gaps and the direction (forward/backward). Do not stop at the first rule that fits two terms; it must fit all terms. In pairs, test the sum 27 first.
