HISTORY · Emergence of Buddhism and Jainism (6th century BCE)

Understand it first

What is it? Around 600–500 BCE two new religious movements, Buddhism (Gautama Buddha) and Jainism (Mahavira), arose in the Ganga plains (Magadha, Kosala, Vajji). They rejected costly Vedic sacrifices and the birth-based caste order and taught a simple path of right conduct. Why did they arise now? Because politics, society, economy and religion had all changed (table below). Because it is asked every year, learn it in the Politics · Society · Economy · Religion order.

Mahabodhi Temple, Bodh Gaya (Bihar): the place where Siddhartha became the Buddha under the peepal tree. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, free licence)

1. Why did new religions arise? (Politics · Society · Economy · Religion)

AreaWhat was happeningEffect
PoliticsTribes had grown into 16 Mahajanapadas; Magadha, Kosala, Vatsa and Avanti were the strongest. Kings and the warrior (Kshatriya) class gained power.Kshatriyas (Buddha and Mahavira were both Kshatriya princes) resented the Brahmins' top place and backed new teachers.
SocietyThe four-varna order had become hereditary and rigid; Shudras and women were low, with no right to study the Vedas.Common people liked teachers who said that a person is great by conduct, not by birth.
EconomyIron ploughs spread farming; cattle were needed for ploughing but sacrifices killed many; towns, trade and punch-marked coins raised the importance of merchants (Vaishyas), who had low rank in the varna order.Merchants and farmers supported non-violent religions that gave them respect (Anathapindika, a banker, gave a monastery to the Buddha).
ReligionVedic religion had become costly, ritual-heavy and in Sanskrit, controlled by priests. The Upanishads already asked deeper questions.People wanted a simple, cheap, local-language path to salvation.

2. Buddhism

Four great places of the Buddha's life
  • Life: Siddhartha Gautama, a prince of the Shakya clan, born in Lumbini (Nepal) about 563 BCE; father Suddhodana, mother Mayadevi (died soon after; raised by Mahaprajapati Gautami). Wife Yashodhara, son Rahula.
  • Four sights (an old man, a sick man, a dead body, a monk) made him ask why people suffer. At 29 he left home: the Great Renunciation (Mahabhinishkramana), with his charioteer Channa and horse Kanthaka. He learnt from Alara Kalama and Uddaka Ramaputta but was not satisfied.
  • Enlightenment (Nirvana/Bodhi): at 35 at Bodh Gaya (Uruvela, on the Niranjana river) under a peepal tree, becoming the 'Buddha' (the awakened one). First sermon at Sarnath (deer park, Isipatana) to five disciples: Dharmachakra Pravartana. Death (Mahaparinirvana) at Kushinagar about 483 BCE, aged 80.
Dhamek Stupa at Sarnath, near the spot of the Buddha's first sermon. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, free licence)

Acharya Nagarjuna (Madhyamika school, Shunyavada) is linked with Andhra.

3. Jainism

Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara, in an old painting. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, free licence)
  • Jains believe in 24 Tirthankaras ('ford-makers' who show the way across the cycle of rebirth). The 1st is Rishabhanatha (Adinatha), the 23rd Parshvanatha (of Varanasi; four vows: non-violence, truth, non-stealing, non-possession), the 24th Mahavira, who added the fifth vow: celibacy (brahmacharya).
  • Mahavira (Vardhamana): born about 599 BCE at Kundagrama near Vaishali in the Jnatrika clan; father Siddhartha, mother Trishala (a Lichchhavi princess); wife Yashoda. Left home at 30, 12 years of penance, attained Kaivalya (perfect knowledge) at Jrimbhikagrama on the Rijupalika river under a sal tree, hence Jina (conqueror) and 'Jainism'. Died at Pavapuri (near Rajagriha) about 527 BCE, aged 72.
IdeaMeaning
Triratna (three jewels)Right faith, right knowledge, right conduct: the path to moksha.
Five vows (Mahavratas)Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Aparigraha, Brahmacharya. Ahimsa is extreme: even insects are protected.
Core ideasEvery living thing and even objects have a soul (jiva); karma sticks to the soul; no creator God; Anekantavada (truth has many sides) and Syadvada ('maybe', perhaps).
SectsDigambara (sky-clad, no clothes; led by Bhadrabahu, who went south with Chandragupta Maurya, to Shravanabelagola) and Svetambara (white-clad; led by Sthulabhadra, Magadha).
Canon and languagePrakrit (Ardhamagadhi); scriptures 12 Angas, compiled in the Council of Pataliputra (c. 300 BCE, Sthulabhadra) and finalised at the Council of Valabhi (c. 512 CE, Devardhi Kshamashramana).
Patrons and placesBimbisara, Ajatashatru, Chandragupta Maurya, Kharavela of Kalinga (Hathigumpha inscription); Gangas of Karnataka (Gomateshwara/Bahubali statue, Shravanabelagola, built by Chamundaraya); Dilwara temples, Mount Abu.

Exam trap

Both were Kshatriya reformers of Magadha region and both rejected Vedic authority and animal sacrifice. Buddha died at Kushinagar, Mahavira at Pavapuri. Buddha's first sermon: Sarnath; his enlightenment: Bodh Gaya. Mahavira added the fifth vow (Parshvanatha preached four). The Buddhist canon is the Tripitaka (Pali), the Jain canon the 12 Angas (Prakrit). Hinayana/Mahayana split: 4th Council, Kanishka. Buddha's symbol of birth: lotus; Mahavira's: lion.

POLITY · The Preamble

Understand it first

What is it? The Preamble is the short introduction at the start of the Constitution. It tells who made it (the people), what kind of state we are building (sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic) and why (justice, liberty, equality, fraternity). It comes from the Objectives Resolution moved by Nehru (Day 1). Think of it as the Constitution's identity card.

The Preamble of the Constitution of India. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, free licence)

1. The words, one by one

WordMeaningExample / note
We, the people of IndiaFinal authority is the people; the Constitution is not a gift of any king or foreign power.Adopted on 26 Nov 1949.
SovereignIndia is free from any outside control and decides its internal and foreign affairs.Yet India stays in the Commonwealth or UN willingly.
Socialist (42nd Amend. 1976)Democratic socialism: the State works to reduce inequality, with a mixed economy (public and private).Not Soviet-type nationalisation of everything.
Secular (42nd Amend. 1976)The State has no official religion and treats all faiths equally; every person is free to follow any religion.Arts 25–28 give the freedom of religion.
DemocraticPower comes from the people through elections (political, social and economic democracy).Universal adult franchise.
RepublicThe head of State (President) is elected, not hereditary.UK is a monarchy; India is a republic.
Four objectives of the Preamble (JLEF)

Memory: J-L-E-F = Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity. The words 'unity and integrity of the nation' in Fraternity were added by the 42nd Amendment. Justice was inspired by the Russian Revolution; Liberty, Equality and Fraternity by the French Revolution (Ireland and USSR ideas also).

2. Is the Preamble part of the Constitution? (cases)

CaseDecision
Berubari Union case (1960)Preamble is not part of the Constitution; it is a key to the mind of the makers.
Kesavananda Bharati (1973)Overruled it: Preamble is part of the Constitution; it can be amended but the basic structure cannot be destroyed.
LIC of India case (1995)Reaffirmed that the Preamble is an integral part.
  • Amended only once: by the 42nd Amendment (1976), which added 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity'.
  • The Preamble is not enforceable in a court, is not a source of power to the legislature and not a limit on its powers. It helps to interpret doubtful provisions.
  • Praise for the Preamble: K.M. Munshi called it the 'political horoscope' of the Constitution; Ernest Barker the 'key-note'; Thakur Das Bhargava the 'soul'.
Example

Example: if a State declared one religion as the 'official religion' and made its rituals compulsory in government schools, it would violate the Preamble's Secular idea (and Arts 25–28). If a law gave an advantage only to people of one caste in public jobs without reason, it would violate Equality of status and opportunity.

Exam trap

Preamble amended once (1976). Berubari: not part; Kesavananda: part. Words added in 1976: Socialist, Secular, Integrity. Republic = elected head. Preamble is non-justiciable. 'We, the people' = sovereignty of the people. The date in the Preamble is 26 Nov 1949, not 26 Jan 1950.

REASONING · Coding–decoding

Understand it first

What is it? A word, number or message is changed into a secret form by a hidden rule. You must find the rule and apply it to a new word. Use the letter positions (A=1 … Z=26) from Day 2. Typical types: letter shifting, opposite (reverse) letters, changing shift, number coding, substitution (word changed with another word) and group codes (find a word's code by comparing sentences).

TypeExampleMethod and answer
Same shiftCAT → ECV; DOG → ?Each letter +2: D→F, O→Q, G→I: FQI
Opposite lettersTABLE → GZYOV; CHAIR → ?Opposite = 27 − position: C→X, H→S, A→Z, I→R, R→I: XSZRI
Changing shiftFRIEND → HUMJTK; CANDLE → ?Shifts +2, +3, +4, +5, +6, +7: C→E, A→D, N→R, D→I, L→R, E→L: EDRIRL
Number codingA=2, B=4, C=6 … (twice the position). Value of FACE?F=12, A=2, C=6, E=10: 30
SubstitutionIf red is called blue, blue is green, green is yellow: colour of the sky?The sky is really blue; blue is called green: green. Always start from the real object.
Group code'sun moon star' = ka mi zo; 'moon sky cloud' = zo ta pe. Code for 'moon'?The common word moon ↔ common code zo
Example

Steps: (1) Write positions above both words. (2) Find the change letter by letter (+, −, 27 − x). (3) If the change differs, look for a pattern in the changes. (4) Apply to the new word and check by decoding one letter back.

Exam trap

In group codes, only the word and code that appear in both sentences can be matched first. In opposite-letter codes, positions add to 27. In substitution, answer using the new name for the real thing.
