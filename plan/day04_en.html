HISTORY · The Mauryan Empire: administration, society and Ashoka

Understand it first

What is it? The Mauryan Empire (about 321–185 BCE) was the first empire that united most of India under one ruler, from Afghanistan in the north-west to Karnataka in the south and from Gujarat to Odisha. Its capital was Pataliputra (Patna). Why did it matter? It created a large, paid bureaucracy, a standing army and a tax system, and under Ashoka it tried to rule by Dhamma (ethical duty) instead of only force. In the exam, learn it as Sources → Rulers → Politics (administration) → Society → Economy → Religion → Art → Decline.

Map of the Mauryan Empire at its greatest extent under Ashoka. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, free licence)

1. How do we know? The sources

SourceWhat it tells
Arthashastra (Kautilya, also called Chanakya or Vishnugupta)A book on statecraft, administration, taxes, spies, law. Gives the Saptanga theory of the state. Its date and authorship are debated, but it describes Mauryan-type administration.
Indica (Megasthenes)Megasthenes was the Greek ambassador of Seleucus Nicator at Chandragupta's court. His book is lost and survives only in quotations of later Greek writers. Describes Pataliputra, the army and city boards.
Mudrarakshasa (Vishakhadatta)A Sanskrit play on how Chanakya helped Chandragupta overthrow the Nandas (written much later).
Ashokan inscriptionsThe most reliable source. Mostly in Brahmi script and Prakrit; in the north-west in Kharosthi, and also Greek and Aramaic. Brahmi was deciphered by James Prinsep in 1837. Ashoka calls himself Devanampiya Piyadasi; the name 'Ashoka' appears in the Maski (Karnataka) and Gujarra minor edicts.
OthersBuddhist books (Mahavamsa, Dipavamsa, Divyavadana), Puranas, Jain books; Junagadh (Girnar) inscription of Rudradaman: Chandragupta's governor Pushyagupta built the Sudarshana lake; Ashoka's governor Tushaspa added canals.

Ashoka's rock edicts at Junagadh (Girnar), Gujarat: the same rock also carries later inscriptions of Rudradaman. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, free licence)

3. Politics: how the empire was ruled

Saptanga (seven limbs of the state, Arthashastra): Swami (king), Amatya (ministers), Janapada (territory and people), Durga (fort), Kosha (treasury), Danda (army), Mitra (ally). The king was the head of administration, army and justice, but his power was meant to serve the people's welfare.

LevelOfficials and details
CentreKing + Mantriparishad (council of ministers) and Amatyas. Arthashastra lists 18 Tirthas (high officers: Mantri, Purohita, Senapati, Yuvaraja…). Many Adhyakshas (superintendents) ran departments: agriculture (Sitadhyaksha), trade, weights, mines, ships, cows, slaves, etc.
ProvinceFour provinces under Kumaras (princes) or Aryaputras: Uttarapatha (Taxila), Avantirashtra (Ujjain), Dakshinapatha (Suvarnagiri) and Prachya/Kalinga (Tosali); Magadha at the centre.

4. Ashoka and Dhamma

  • What is Dhamma? Not a new religion. It is an ethical code for all people: tolerance of all sects, non-violence to living beings, respect for parents, elders and teachers, kindness to servants and slaves, generosity to Brahmins and ascetics, truthfulness. Ashoka said it in simple words so that every subject could follow it.
  • How he spread it: Dhamma Mahamatras (appointed in the 13th year of his reign), Dhammayatras (tours instead of hunting trips), Rajukas and Pradeshikas touring every 5 years, edicts in local places, hospitals and wells, trees along roads, bans on animal sacrifice and some hunting.
  • Buddhism: convened the Third Council at Pataliputra; sent missions abroad, his son Mahendra and daughter Sanghamitra to Sri Lanka. Rock Edict XIII names Greek kings (Antiochus, Ptolemy, Antigonus, Magas, Alexander) he sent envoys to.
Type of edictDetails
Major Rock Edicts (14)Girnar, Kalsi, Dhauli, Jaugada, Shahbazgarhi, Mansehra, Sopara, Erragudi and others. Edict XIII: Kalinga war.
Minor Rock EdictsMaski, Gujarra (name 'Ashoka'), Brahmagiri, Siddapura, Jatinga-Rameshwara, Rajula Mandagiri.
Pillar Edicts (7)Delhi-Topra, Lauriya Nandangarh, Allahabad (Kausambi), Sarnath, Sanchi. The Lumbini (Rummindei) pillar records Ashoka's visit and a tax reduction for the village.
The Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath: India's national emblem (adopted 26 Jan 1950); its Dharma Chakra is on the national flag. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, free licence)
Example

Andhra link (all regions equal): Ashokan edicts in Andhra Pradesh are at Erragudi and Rajula Mandagiri (Kurnool district) and a Mauryan-period presence at Amaravati (Guntur) and Buddhist sites of the coast. The Mauryan southern limit was roughly the Penna region (Brahmagiri, Siddapura, Maski in Karnataka). Ashoka's thirteenth edict mentions the Andhras among the peoples inside his empire.

5. Politics · Society · Economy · Religion of the Mauryas

AreaMauryan period
PoliticsCentralised bureaucratic monarchy; paid officials; spies; four provinces; Ashoka's paternal rule ('all men are my children'); the first Indian empire.
SocietyVarna society with Brahmins and Kshatriyas on top; Megasthenes wrongly saw 7 classes (philosophers, farmers, soldiers, herdsmen, artisans, magistrates, councillors) and said there was no slavery, while the Arthashastra describes dasas. Women: ganikas (courtesans), spies and royal guards; guilds (shrenis) of craftsmen.
EconomyState control of land, mines, salt, trade. Taxes: bhaga (about 1/6 of produce), bali, tolls, sale tax. Silver punch-marked coins (pana/karshapana) checked by Lakshanadhyaksha/rupyadarsaka. Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW) pottery. Trade routes Uttarapatha (Taxila to Pataliputra) and Dakshinapatha; ports Tamralipti, Bharukachchha, Sopara. Forest and irrigation (Sudarshana lake).
ReligionChandragupta Jain, Bindusara Ajivika, Ashoka Buddhist but tolerant: he gave the Barabar caves to Ajivikas. Brahmanical sacrifices were discouraged by Ashoka. Dhamma was a common ethical code.

6. Mauryan art and decline

  • Court art: polished stone pillars of Chunar sandstone with animal capitals (lion, bull, elephant), e.g. Sarnath lion capital, Rampurva bull, Lauriya Nandangarh lion; Didarganj Yakshi (Patna Museum); Dhauli elephant (Odisha). Rock-cut caves: Barabar and Nagarjuni hills (Bihar), the Lomas Rishi cave. Stupas: the original Sanchi stupa brick core is attributed to Ashoka.
  • Why did it decline? (1) weak successors after Ashoka; (2) cost of a huge army and bureaucracy (financial strain, shown by debased coins); (3) very large empire with poor communication; (4) oppression by officials in provinces (Taxila revolts); (5) Brahmin dissatisfaction with Ashoka's policy, in one theory; (6) Greek invasions from the north-west; (7) the coup of Pushyamitra Shunga. No single cause explains it.
Exam trap

Arthashastra: Kautilya; Indica: Megasthenes (lost, only quotations); Mudrarakshasa: Vishakhadatta (not Kalhana, who wrote Rajatarangini). Brahmi deciphered by Prinsep, 1837. Rock Edict XIII = Kalinga war; the Sarnath Lion Capital = national emblem. Chandragupta: Jain; Bindusara: Ajivika; Ashoka: Buddhist (Third Council, Pataliputra). Seleucus defeated 305 BCE. Last Mauryan killed in 185 BCE. Tripitaka are Buddhist, not Mauryan administrative texts.

POLITY · Fundamental Rights I: Articles 12–19

Understand it first

What is it? Fundamental Rights (FRs) are basic freedoms and protections in Part III (Articles 12–35) that the State cannot take away. They are called 'fundamental' because they are enforceable in the Supreme Court and High Courts (unlike Directive Principles). They came from the USA (Bill of Rights); the Constitution lists six groups. Today we study Articles 12–19: what 'State' means, what happens to laws that break FRs, Right to Equality and the six freedoms.

The six Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35)

Property was a Fundamental Right (Art 19(1)(f) and Art 31) until the 44th Amendment (1978) removed it. It is now only a legal right under Article 300A.

1. Articles 12 and 13: the base

ArticleMeaningKey points and cases
Art 12: 'State'The rights are claimed against the 'State'. It includes the Government and Parliament of India, the Government and Legislature of each State, local authorities and other authorities.'Other authorities' (bodies like electricity boards and public corporations) were explained in Ajay Hasia (1981). A purely private person is generally not 'State'.
Art 13: laws against FRs are voidAny law (including ordinances, orders, bye-laws, customs) that goes against an FR is void to that extent. This gives the courts the power of judicial review.Doctrine of eclipse: an old law that conflicts with an FR is not dead, it is only 'covered' (dormant) and revives if the conflict is removed. Severability: only the bad part is struck down. Art 13(4) (24th Amendment, 1971): Article 13 does not apply to constitutional amendments under Art 368; later limited by the basic structure doctrine (Kesavananda 1973).

2. Right to Equality: Articles 14–18

ArticleWhat it saysKey points
Art 14Equality before law (British idea: no one is above the law) and equal protection of the laws (American idea: equals treated equally).Reasonable classification is allowed (different rules for different groups if the difference is related to the aim). Exceptions: Art 361 (President and Governors not answerable to courts for official acts). Applies to all persons.
Art 15No discrimination only on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth; no restriction on access to shops, wells, roads, etc.Exceptions: 15(3) special provisions for women and children; 15(4) for socially and educationally backward classes, SCs and STs (added by the 1st Amendment 1951, after the Champakam Dorairajan case); 15(5) (93rd Amendment 2005) reservation in educational institutions; 15(6) (103rd Amendment 2019) 10% for economically weaker sections (EWS), upheld by the Supreme Court in 2022 (3:2).
Art 16Equality of opportunity in public employment: no discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth or residence.16(4) reservation for backward classes; 16(4A) in promotions for SC/ST (77th Amendment 1995); 16(4B) carry-forward of vacancies (81st, 2000); 16(6) EWS. Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992, Mandal case): upheld 27% OBC reservation, set a 50% ceiling, excluded the creamy layer, and said no reservation in promotions (later changed by the 77th Amendment).
Art 17Abolition of untouchability; its practice in any form is an offence.Laws: Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955 and the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989. Note: 'untouchability' is not defined in the Constitution.
Art 18Abolition of titles: the State cannot give titles (except military and academic distinctions); citizens cannot accept foreign titles; a foreigner holding office cannot accept one without the President's consent.Bharat Ratna, Padma awards are not 'titles' (Balaji Raghavan case, 1996), so they are allowed, but they must not be used as a prefix or suffix to the name.

3. Right to Freedom: Article 19 (six freedoms)

Article 19 gives citizens only (not foreigners or companies) six freedoms. A seventh, to hold property (19(1)(f)), was deleted in 1978. All six can be limited by reasonable restrictions.

ClauseFreedomNote
19(1)(a)Speech and expressionIncludes press freedom, right to know, to remain silent, to broadcast; Romesh Thappar (1950); Shreya Singhal (2015) struck down Section 66A of the IT Act.
19(1)(b)Assemble peacefully and without armsRestriction: public order (Section 144 CrPC type orders).
19(1)(c)Form associations, unions or cooperative societies'Cooperative societies' added by the 97th Amendment 2011.
19(1)(d)Move freely throughout IndiaCan be restricted for the interest of the general public and protection of Scheduled Tribes.
19(1)(e)Reside and settle in any part of IndiaSame restrictions as (d).
19(1)(g)Practise any profession, or carry on any occupation, trade or businessThe State may prescribe qualifications or reserve some trades for itself.

Grounds for restriction (19(2)): sovereignty and integrity of India, security of the State, friendly relations with foreign States, public order (added by the 1st Amendment 1951), decency or morality, contempt of court, defamation, incitement to an offence. During a national emergency on the ground of war or external aggression, the Article 19 freedoms are suspended automatically (Art 358).

Example

Example: a district officer bans a peaceful meeting of farmers saying 'public order' but shows no actual threat. This affects Art 19(1)(b). The restriction must be reasonable and based on a real ground in 19(3); court review can strike it down. If the same farmers form a cooperative society, Art 19(1)(c) protects it.

Exam trap

Art 14 applies to all persons; Art 19 only to citizens. Property: deleted from FRs by the 44th Amendment (1978), now Art 300A. Cooperatives: 97th Amendment. EWS 10%: 103rd Amendment (Art 15(6), 16(6)). Indra Sawhney: 50% ceiling and creamy layer. Public order was added to 19(2) by the 1st Amendment. Art 17 does not define untouchability.

REASONING · Blood relations

Understand it first

What is it? A blood-relation question gives a few facts such as 'A is the brother of B' and asks how two persons are related. The secret: do not solve it in your head. Draw a small family tree: use + for male, − for female, = for married, a horizontal line for brothers and sisters, and a vertical line for parent to child. Then read the link between the two persons.

RelationName
Father's or mother's father/motherGrandfather / Grandmother
Father's brotherPaternal uncle (chacha)
Mother's brotherMaternal uncle (mama)
Father's sister / Mother's sisterPaternal aunt (bua) / Maternal aunt (mausi)
Children of uncle or auntCousins
Brother's or sister's son / daughterNephew / Niece
Spouse's father / motherFather-in-law / Mother-in-law
Spouse's brother / sister, sister's husbandBrother-in-law / Sister-in-law

TypeExampleSolution
Direct chainA is the brother of B. B is the sister of C. C is the father of D. How is A related to D?A, B, C are siblings; C is D's father, so A is D's father's brother: paternal uncle.
'Pointing to' typePointing to a man, a woman says, 'His mother is the only daughter of my mother.' How is the woman related to the man?My mother's only daughter = myself. So his mother is me: she is his mother.
Coded relation'A + B' means A is the mother of B; 'A − B' means A is the brother of B. In 'P + Q − R', how is R related to P?P is the mother of Q; Q is the brother of R. So R is P's child (son or daughter).
Exam trap

Do not assume gender: 'cousin', 'sibling', 'spouse', 'child' do not tell male or female. If the gender cannot be found, the answer may be 'cannot be determined'. 'Only son' or 'only daughter' limits the number of children, which is useful for the chain.
