HISTORY · Mughal Empire: administration, society, economy

Understand it first

What is it? The Mughals (1526–1857; real power until 1707) built the largest and richest empire in medieval India. Their great gift was an administrative system (mansabdari, provinces, land revenue) that later rulers, including the British, copied. They also joined Persian and Indian styles in art and architecture and, under Akbar, tried a policy of religious tolerance. Learn it as Sources → Rulers → Administration → Politics · Society · Economy · Religion → Art and architecture → Decline.

The Mansabdari system

  • Mansab = rank. Introduced by Akbar (c. 1570–71); every state officer, civil or military, held a mansab. Rank by zat (personal rank, which fixed pay and status) and sawar (the number of cavalrymen he had to keep). Ranks from 10 to 5,000 for nobles (later to 7,000 and 10,000; princes up to 12,000). Three groups: mansabdar (up to 500 zat), amir (500–2,500), amir-i-umda (above 2,500).
  • Not hereditary; the emperor appointed, promoted, demoted and transferred them. Pay: in cash (naqdi) or by a jagir (assignment of the revenue of a region, not ownership; changed every 2–3 years so nobles could not build local power). Dagh (branding horses) and chehra (descriptive roll) checked fraud. Sawar ranks: if 'sawar' was equal to zat = first class; half or less = second/third class.
  • Later problem: by Aurangzeb there were too many mansabdars and too few jagirs (jagirdari crisis, bejagiri), so nobles squeezed the peasants and the army weakened.

Land revenue

SystemHow it worked
Zabt / Dahsala (Todar Mal; 1580)Land was measured (with a jarib, bamboo joined by iron rings) and classed as Polaj (cultivated every year), Parauti (fallow 1–2 years), Chachar (fallow 3–4) and Banjar (uncultivated 5+). The average yield and prices of the last ten years were used to fix the state's share: one-third of the average produce, paid in cash, in a fixed rate per bigha for each crop in each area (dastur). Used mainly in Delhi, Agra, Lahore, Multan, Malwa.
Batai / GhallabakshiCrop-sharing at harvest; Kankut (estimate of the standing crop); Nasaq (an estimate from past records).

5. Decline

Causes: the jagirdari crisis, Aurangzeb's Deccan wars and religious policy (jizya, conflict with Rajputs, Sikhs and Marathas), peasant revolts, weak successors and noble factions, Nadir Shah (1739) and Abdali (1761), the rise of regional states (Awadh, Bengal, Hyderabad, Marathas) and finally the East India Company (Plassey 1757, Buxar 1764).

POLITY · Legislature, Executive and Judiciary; unicameral and bicameral legislatures

Understand it first

What is it? A government has three jobs: make the laws (legislature), carry them out (executive), and judge disputes and test whether laws follow the Constitution (judiciary). The French thinker Montesquieu said these should be in different hands so that no one gets too much power. India follows this in spirit but not in a strict way.

Three organs of government
The new Parliament building (Sansad Bhavan), New Delhi, where the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha meet. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, free licence)

1. Separation of powers in India

  • Not a strict separation (unlike the USA). The Supreme Court said in Ram Jawaya Kapur (1955) that India does not have a rigid separation, but it has a separation of functions. The overlaps: ministers (executive) are members of Parliament and answer to it; the President is part of Parliament and the head of the executive; the legislature can impeach judges and amend the Constitution; the judiciary makes law through interpretation.
  • Checks and balances: judicial review (Arts 13, 32, 226); Parliament controls the executive by questions, motions, committees and no-confidence; the executive can issue ordinances (Art 123) and appoints judges; Art 50 directs the State to separate the judiciary from the executive in public services; Art 121 bars discussion of a judge's conduct in Parliament. Separation of powers is held to be part of the basic structure.

2. The Legislature: unicameral and bicameral

A legislature with one house is unicameral; with two houses it is bicameral. Why two? A second house gives second thought, represents States or experts and prevents hasty laws. Why one? It is simpler, cheaper and avoids delay. Parliament is bicameral (Lok Sabha = lower house of the people; Rajya Sabha = upper house of the States). Parliament = President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha (Art 79).

State legislatures

  • Every State has a Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha, directly elected, 60 to 500 members, 5 years). Art 168 allows a second house, the Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad): only six States have one: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Bihar and Uttar Pradesh. (J&K's was abolished in 2019.)
  • Creation or abolition (Art 169): the Assembly passes a resolution by a special majority (majority of total members and 2/3 present and voting), then Parliament passes a law by simple majority. It is not an amendment under Art 368. (The Andhra Pradesh Assembly's 2020 resolution to abolish its Council was later withdrawn.)
  • Size of Council: not more than one-third of the Assembly and not less than 40. Composition: 1/3 elected by MLAs; 1/3 by local bodies (municipalities, panchayats); 1/12 by graduates; 1/12 by teachers; 1/6 nominated by the Governor (art, science, literature, social service). Council is permanent, one-third retire every 2 years, term 6 years; it can only delay an ordinary Bill (up to 3 months, then 1 month second time); Money Bills: only the Assembly decides.

4. The Judiciary

  • India has a single integrated judiciary: Supreme Court (Art 124; 34 judges including the CJI) at the top, then 25 High Courts (Art 214), then district and subordinate courts (Arts 233–237). (The USA has separate federal and State courts.)
  • Independence: judges are appointed by the President after consulting the collegium (Second Judges case 1993); security of tenure (removal only by impeachment, Art 124(4), by special majority in both Houses); salaries charged on the Consolidated Fund; no discussion of conduct in Parliament (Art 121); power to punish for contempt; no practice after retirement in the same court.
  • Functions: original, appellate and advisory (Art 143) jurisdiction; judicial review; guardian of the Constitution and Fundamental Rights (Arts 32, 226); Lok Adalats and tribunals give quick justice.

Understand it first

What is it? Everything in arithmetic rests on numbers, so first learn the families of numbers and their properties (even, odd, prime, factors), then the rules that speed up calculations (divisibility), and finally how to judge the size of a number (order of magnitude), which helps you estimate answers quickly in the exam.

3. Factors and divisors

Example

Write N as a product of primes: N = a^p × b^q × c^r. Then: number of divisors = (p+1)(q+1)(r+1); sum of divisors = (a⁰+…+a^p)(b⁰+…+b^q)(c⁰+…+c^r). A number has an odd number of divisors only if it is a perfect square. A number with exactly 3 divisors is the square of a prime. Example: 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5: divisors = 4 × 3 × 2 = 24; sum = 15 × 13 × 6 = 1,170; odd divisors come from 3² × 5 only: 3 × 2 = 6, so even divisors = 24 − 6 = 18. HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers.

5. Order of magnitude

Understand it first

Meaning: the order of magnitude tells how big a number is in powers of 10. Write the number as a × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ a < 10; n is its order of magnitude (a stricter physics rule rounds up to n + 1 when a is more than about 3.16, but for the exam use the simple rule). Shortcut: order = (number of digits) − 1 for numbers 1 or more. It lets you compare huge numbers and check whether an answer is sensible.

Example

Using it: a ratio of two numbers has order = difference of the orders. India ≈ 10⁹ and a State ≈ 10⁷: ratio ≈ 10², and exactly 1.4 × 10⁹ ÷ 5 × 10⁷ = 28, between 10¹ and 10², about 10¹. Estimating: 49 × 51 ≈ 50 × 50 = 2,500 (order 3); 3,98,000 ÷ 19,900 ≈ 4,00,000 ÷ 20,000 = 20. When options are far apart, estimation alone finds the answer.

Exam trap

1 is neither prime nor composite; 2 is the only even prime; 25 primes below 100; divisors count = (p+1)(q+1)…; 3 divisors ⇒ square of a prime; HCF × LCM = product; 0.̅x = x/9; 1 crore = 10 million = 10⁷; 1 lakh crore = 1 trillion; order of magnitude = (digits − 1).
