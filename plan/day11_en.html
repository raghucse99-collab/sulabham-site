HISTORY · Bhakti and Sufi movements

Understand it first

What is it? Bhakti means loving devotion to a personal God; Sufism is the mystical side of Islam, the search for union with God through love. Both grew in medieval India between about the 6th and 17th centuries, when rigid ritual, caste and priestly control troubled ordinary people. Both said that God can be reached through love, not through ceremony, birth or learning, both used local languages and music, and both shaped Indian society, language and religious harmony. Learn it as Bhakti (south → north → west) → Sufism (orders, ideas) → Sikhism → Impact.

The Bhakti movement
A Kangra painting of Mirabai, the Krishna-devotee saint-poet of Rajasthan. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, free licence)

1. Bhakti in the south (6th–13th century)

GroupFacts
Alvars (Vishnu)12 saints; hymns in Tamil collected as the Nalayira Divya Prabandham. Nammalvar, Tirumangai, Periyalvar, Andal (the woman Alvar, sang of her love for Vishnu: *Tiruppavai*).
Nayanars (Shiva)63 saints; hymns collected as the Tevaram (Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar) and Tirumurai; life stories in Periyapuranam (Sekkizhar). Karaikkal Ammaiyar, a woman saint.
Philosophers (acharyas)Shankara (8th c., Advaita, non-dualism); Ramanuja (11th–12th c., Vishishtadvaita, qualified non-dualism, *Sri Bhashya*, Sri Vaishnavism); Madhva (13th c., Dvaita, dualism); Nimbarka (Dvaitadvaita); Vallabha (Shuddhadvaita, Pushtimarg).
Basavanna (12th c., Karnataka)Minister of Bijjala at Kalyana; founded the Veerashaiva / Lingayat movement; Anubhava Mantapa (hall of experience); wrote Vachanas in Kannada; opposed caste, idol worship and rituals; saints Akka Mahadevi, Allama Prabhu.

2. Bhakti in the north

SaintFacts
Ramananda (14th–15th c., Banaras)Devotee of Rama; taught in Hindi, not Sanskrit; accepted disciples of all castes: Kabir (weaver), Ravidas (cobbler), Sena (barber), Dhanna (Jat), Pipa (Rajput).
Kabir (c. 1440–1518)Nirguna (formless) God; Hindu–Muslim unity; attacked idol worship, caste, pilgrimage, empty ritual; compositions: dohas, sakhis, sabads, ramainis, collected in the Bijak; followers: Kabirpanthis.
Ravidas (Raidas)Cobbler-saint of Banaras; nirguna devotion; verses included in the Guru Granth Sahib; Ravidassia community.
Guru Nanak (1469–1539)Born at Talwandi (Nankana Sahib); one formless God (Ik Onkar), *Japji*; rejected idol worship, caste and rituals; started langar (community kitchen), sangat (congregation) and pangat (eating together); settled at Kartarpur; made udasis (journeys).
Chaitanya (1486–1534)Born at Nadia (Bengal); Krishna devotion (Gaudiya Vaishnavism); sankirtan (congregational singing); Achintya Bheda-Abheda philosophy; spread the movement to Puri (Odisha) and Vrindavan.
Tulsidas (1532–1623)Banaras; Rama devotion (saguna); Ramcharitmanas in Awadhi (Akbar's time); also *Vinaya Patrika*, *Kavitavali*.
Surdas (c. 1483–1563)Blind poet; Krishna devotion; Sur Sagar; disciple of Vallabha; Braj language; lived near Agra.
Mirabai (16th c.)Rathor princess of Merta (Rajasthan), wife of Bhoj Raj of Mewar; devotion to Krishna; songs in Rajasthani/Braj; defied family custom; considered Ravidas her guru.
Sankaradeva (1449–1568, Assam)Ekasarana dharma (devotion to one God, Krishna); Namghar (prayer hall); Kirtan Ghosa; Ankiya Nat (one-act plays); Sattras (monasteries).

4. Teachings of Bhakti

  • One God, reached through love and surrender, not rituals, sacrifices or priests. Nirguna (God without form: Kabir, Nanak) and Saguna (God with form: Rama, Krishna: Tulsidas, Surdas, Mirabai).
  • Against caste and untouchability; saints came from all castes; women saints (Andal, Akka Mahadevi, Mirabai, Janabai); the guru is essential; singing (kirtan, bhajan) in the local language.

POLITY · Parliament: composition, powers, Bills and the Budget

Understand it first

What is it? Parliament is the law-making and money-controlling body of the Union. This day goes deeper than Day 9: who can sit in it, how it works (sessions, motions, committees), how a Bill becomes law, and how the Budget is passed. The big idea: the government can spend or tax only if Parliament permits it (Art 265: *no tax except by authority of law*; Art 266: no money from the Consolidated Fund except by law).

1. Composition, membership and the Speaker

PointDetails
StrengthLok Sabha: maximum 550 (530 from States, 20 from UTs) after the 104th Amendment (2019) ended Anglo-Indian nomination (earlier 552); 543 elected. Rajya Sabha: maximum 250 (238 + 12 nominated). Seats are fixed by the 1971 Census until the first Census after 2026 (42nd, 84th Amendments); seats are reserved for SCs and STs (Art 330).
Qualifications (Art 84)Citizen of India; 25 years (Lok Sabha), 30 years (Rajya Sabha); other qualifications by the Representation of the People Act, 1951; must take an oath.
Disqualification (Art 102)Office of profit under the Government; unsound mind declared by a court; undischarged insolvent; not a citizen or acquired foreign citizenship; disqualified by law; and for defection under the Tenth Schedule.
Anti-defection (Tenth Schedule)Added by the 52nd Amendment (1985); a member is disqualified if he voluntarily gives up party membership or votes against the party whip; the Presiding Officer decides; Kihoto Hollohan (1992) held that the decision can be reviewed by courts; the 91st Amendment (2003) removed the 'one-third split' exception and kept only the two-thirds merger.
Speaker (Art 93–96)Elected by the Lok Sabha from its members; presides, keeps order, decides if a Bill is a Money Bill (final, Art 110(3)), casting vote only in a tie; resigns to the Deputy Speaker; removed by a resolution of all the then members (effective majority) with 14 days' notice; a pro tem Speaker administers oaths at the start.

2. How Parliament works

FeatureDetails
Sessions (Art 85)Summoned by the President; gap between two sessions not more than 6 months; three sessions: Budget (Feb–May), Monsoon (Jul–Aug), Winter (Nov–Dec); quorum = 1/10 of members (Art 100).
Question Hour, Zero HourQuestion Hour (first hour; starred, unstarred, short notice questions) is a control over the executive; Zero Hour (after Question Hour) is an Indian innovation (not in the rules) for urgent matters.
MotionsAdjournment (urgent public matter; only Lok Sabha; 50 members' support); Calling attention; No-confidence (only Lok Sabha; 50 members' support); Censure; Privilege; Cut motions (policy cut Re 1, economy cut, token cut Rs 100) on demands for grants.
CommitteesPublic Accounts Committee (22: 15 LS + 7 RS; chaired by an Opposition member by convention; checks CAG reports); Estimates Committee (30, only LS); Committee on Public Undertakings (22); Departmentally Related Standing Committees (24, from 1993; 31 members each); Select and Joint committees.
PrivilegesFreedom of speech in Parliament (Art 105), immunity from court proceedings for what is said or voted; arrest protection in civil cases during session; contempt powers.

3. Bills: kinds and passage

How an ordinary Bill becomes law
KindRules
Ordinary Bill (Art 107–108)Introduced in either House; needs a simple majority; Rajya Sabha can amend or reject; deadlock: joint sitting (Art 108); the President may assent, withhold, or return once.
Money Bill (Art 110)Deals only with taxes, borrowing, the Consolidated or Contingency Fund, appropriation and similar matters; only in Lok Sabha, on the President's recommendation; Speaker certifies it (final); Rajya Sabha may only recommend changes within 14 days; Lok Sabha may accept or reject them; if RS does not return it in 14 days it is deemed passed; no joint sitting; the President cannot return it (can only assent or withhold).
Financial Bill (Art 117)Category I: has money provisions plus other matters; only in LS with President's recommendation; joint sitting possible. Category II: only involves expenditure from the Consolidated Fund; either House; cannot be passed unless the President recommends consideration.
Constitution Amendment Bill (Art 368)Either House; special majority: majority of the total membership of each House and 2/3 of members present and voting; for federal features also ratification by at least half of the States; no joint sitting; the President must assent (24th Amendment). Kesavananda Bharati (1973): Parliament cannot change the basic structure.

Stages of an ordinary Bill: (1) First reading: introduction. (2) Second reading: general discussion, then committee stage (clause by clause), then report stage. (3) Third reading: final vote. Then the other House repeats the stages, and the President gives assent. A Bill passed by Parliament lapses only if it is pending in the Lok Sabha when it is dissolved; a Bill pending in the Rajya Sabha but not passed by the Lok Sabha does not lapse.

MATHS · Ratio and proportion

Understand it first

What is it? A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind by division: if a class has 20 boys and 30 girls, boys : girls = 20 : 30 = 2 : 3. It has no unit, and it tells how many times (or what part) one is of the other. A proportion says that two ratios are equal: 2 : 3 = 4 : 6. Almost all of partnership, mixtures, ages, speed–time–work and percentages are ratio problems in disguise, so this topic is a base for the next several days.

Ratio and proportion: the toolkit

1. Ratio

  • In a : b, a is the antecedent and b the consequent. Both quantities must be in the same unit (convert ₹ and paise, or metres and cm, first). Multiply or divide both terms by the same number to get an equal ratio; reduce to the simplest form.
  • Compare: a : b > c : d if ad > bc. Kinds: duplicate a² : b²; triplicate a³ : b³; sub-duplicate √a : √b; inverse b : a; compound ac : bd (multiply the antecedents and the consequents).
  • Dividing a quantity N in the ratio a : b gives aN/(a + b) and bN/(a + b). Joining ratios: if A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5, make B equal (the LCM of 3 and 4 is 12): A : B = 8 : 12 and B : C = 12 : 15, so A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15.
  • Adding the same number to both terms moves the ratio towards 1 : 1: adding 1 to 3 : 5 gives 4 : 6 = 2 : 3 (0.667), nearer to 1 than 3 : 5 (0.6).

2. Proportion

  • a : b = c : d (a, b, c, d in proportion): a and d are the extremes, b and c the means; product of extremes = product of means: ad = bc. This is the cross-multiplication rule used in every rule-of-three problem.
  • Continued proportion a : b = b : c: b² = ac, so b = √(ac) is the mean proportional. Third proportional to a and b: a : b = b : x gives x = b²/a. Fourth proportional to a, b, c: a : b = c : x gives x = bc/a.
Property (if a : b = c : d)Result
Invertendob : a = d : c
Alternendoa : c = b : d
Componendo(a + b) : b = (c + d) : d
Dividendo(a − b) : b = (c − d) : d
Componendo and dividendo(a + b) : (a − b) = (c + d) : (c − d)
Example

Example: if (x + y) : (x − y) = 7 : 3, apply componendo and dividendo: 2x : 2y = (7 + 3) : (7 − 3) = 10 : 4, so x : y = 5 : 2. Variation: direct (y/x constant: more goods, more cost) and inverse (xy constant: more men, fewer days). Partnership: profit is shared in the ratio of (capital × time). If A puts ₹30,000 for 8 months and B ₹40,000 for 6 months, the ratio is 240 : 240 = 1 : 1.

Exam trap

ad = bc; mean proportional = √(ac); third proportional = b²/a; fourth proportional = bc/a; share = (part / total parts) × N; joining ratios: equalise the common term; componendo-dividendo: (a+b)/(a−b) = (c+d)/(c−d); partnership = capital × time.
