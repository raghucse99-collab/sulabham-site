HISTORY · Social and religious reform movements: 20th century

Understand it first

What changed after 1900? The 19th-century reformers were mostly upper-caste, urban and educated, and they asked for reform from above. In the 20th century, the people who suffered most from caste, the depressed classes, non-Brahmins and women, began to organise for themselves. They used satyagraha (mass protest), newspapers, political parties and elections, and demanded rights, not favours: entry to temples, use of public tanks and roads, seats in legislatures and jobs. Reform now joined the national movement and the making of the Constitution (Art 15, 17, reservations). Learn it as Non-Brahmin and temple-entry movements → Ambedkar → Gandhi's work → women's organisations → Telugu lands → Impact.

Key 20th-century movements

1. Non-Brahmin and temple-entry movements in the south

MovementDetails

Madhavan, K. Kelappan; Periyar joined, called 'Vaikom Veerar'; Gandhi visited); Guruvayur Satyagraha (1931–32) (Kelappan; A.K. Gopalan); Travancore Temple Entry Proclamation (1936).

3. Women's organisations and laws

  • Women's Indian Association (1917, Adyar): Annie Besant, Margaret Cousins, Dorothy Jinarajadasa; demanded votes for women. National Council of Women in India (1925); All India Women's Conference (1927): education, then wider reform.
  • Child Marriage Restraint (Sarda) Act, 1929: minimum marriage age 14 for girls and 18 for boys. Dr Muthulakshmi Reddy: India's first woman legislator (Madras, 1927); fought the devadasi system (Madras Devadasis Act, 1947). Durgabai Deshmukh founded the Andhra Mahila Sabha (1937).

POLITY · Right to Information (RTI)

Understand it first

What is it? In a democracy the people are the real masters, so they have a right to know what their government is doing with their money and power. The Right to Information Act, 2005 lets any citizen ask any public authority for information, without giving a reason, and get a reply within a fixed time. It makes government transparent and accountable and helps fight corruption. The Supreme Court had already said the right to know is part of freedom of speech and expression (Art 19(1)(a)); the Act gives it a procedure. As a government employee you will both use it and answer it, so know the sections, time limits and appeals well.

How an RTI request moves

1. Background

  • Courts: State of UP v Raj Narain (1975) and S.P. Gupta (1981): the right to know flows from Art 19(1)(a). Movement: Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan (MKSS), Rajasthan, led by Aruna Roy in the 1990s: public hearings (jan sunwai) on village works. States first: Tamil Nadu and Goa (1997). Freedom of Information Act, 2002 (never came into force). RTI Act passed 15 June 2005, in force fully on 12 October 2005.

2. Important sections

SectionWhat it says
2(f), 2(h), 2(j)Information = any material in any form (records, emails, samples). Public authority = bodies set up by the Constitution, laws or government orders, and bodies (including NGOs) substantially financed by government. Right to information includes inspecting works and records and taking certified copies or samples.
4Proactive (suo motu) disclosure: each authority must publish its functions, officers, budgets, schemes and decisions so that people need not apply.
5, 6Every office names a Public Information Officer (PIO) and an Assistant PIO. Apply in writing or electronically, in English, Hindi or the local official language; fee ₹10 (Centre; States fix their own); BPL applicants pay nothing; no reason need be given.
7Reply within 30 days; 48 hours if it concerns life or liberty; +5 days if filed through an APIO; 40 days if third-party information is involved; no reply = deemed refusal.
8, 9, 11Exemptions (Sec 8): information harming sovereignty, security, strategic or economic interests, forbidden by courts, breaching Parliament's privilege, trade secrets, foreign governments' confidential information, endangering life, impeding investigation, Cabinet papers (until the decision is taken), and personal information (Sec 8(1)(j), widened by the DPDP Act, 2023). Most exempt matters can be released after 20 years (Sec 8(3)). Sec 9: copyright of a private person. Sec 11: third-party information needs notice to the third party.
19First appeal to an officer senior to the PIO in the same office within 30 days (decided in 30, at most 45 days); second appeal to the Central or State Information Commission within 90 days.
20Penalty on the PIO for delay or refusal without reason: ₹250 a day, up to ₹25,000; the Commission may also recommend disciplinary action.
24Intelligence and security organisations in the Second Schedule (e.g. IB, R&AW, BSF, CRPF, NSG) are outside the Act, except information on corruption and human rights violations (human rights information needs the Commission's approval and is given within 45 days).

3. Information Commissions

  • Central Information Commission (CIC): Chief Information Commissioner + up to 10 Information Commissioners; appointed by the President on a committee of the PM, Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha and a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the PM. The RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019 let the Central Government fix their term and salary (the rules set 3 years; earlier it was 5 years or 65). Removal by the President after a Supreme Court inquiry (or on other stated grounds).
  • State Information Commission (SIC): appointed by the Governor on a committee of the Chief Minister, Leader of the Opposition and a Cabinet Minister. Supreme Court (2019): the office of the Chief Justice of India is a public authority under the RTI Act.
Exam trap

SCIENCE · National S&T and innovation policy, national missions and emerging technologies

Understand it first

What is it? A country grows rich and secure when it can create new knowledge and technology itself instead of importing it. So the Government sets a policy (the broad aim), funds research, and runs missions: time-bound programmes with a clear target and budget (for example, '5 million tonnes of green hydrogen a year by 2030'). Emerging technologies are new fields, such as AI, quantum and semiconductors, that will decide jobs, defence and the economy in the coming decades. Exams ask about the year, ministry, target and budget of each mission, and what a technology does. Some figures change with new budgets, so check current affairs before the exam.
