HISTORY · National Movement IV: Cripps, Quit India, INA, Cabinet Mission

Understand it first

What is it? The Second World War (1939–45) changed everything. Britain dragged India into the war without asking Indians, needed Indian help, and came out of the war weak. Indians now demanded freedom, not reforms: through the Quit India Movement (1942), the last great mass struggle, and through armed struggle by Netaji's Indian National Army. Britain's offers (Cripps 1942, Cabinet Mission 1946) show its move from delay to withdrawal, while the Muslim League's demand for Pakistan grew. Learn it as war and the first offers → Cripps → Quit India → INA → 1945–46 (Simla, elections, mutinies) → Cabinet Mission → road to 1947.

From the war to the Cabinet Mission

1. The war years, 1939–41

  • 3 September 1939: Viceroy Linlithgow declared India at war without consulting Indian leaders; the Congress ministries resigned (October–November 1939); the Muslim League celebrated a 'Day of Deliverance' (22 December 1939).
  • Lahore Resolution (23 March 1940): the Muslim League demanded separate 'independent States' for Muslim-majority areas (the 'Pakistan Resolution', moved by Fazlul Huq). August Offer (8 August 1940): dominion status as the aim (no date), more Indians in the Viceroy's Council, and a veto for minorities on any future constitution; rejected. Individual Satyagraha (October 1940): chosen individuals protested against the war; first Vinoba Bhave, second Jawaharlal Nehru.

2. The Cripps Mission (March 1942)

  • Why: Japan was advancing (Singapore fell, Rangoon fell, March 1942) and Britain needed Indian support; the USA and China also pressed Britain. Sir Stafford Cripps offered: dominion status after the war; a constitution-making body elected by provincial assemblies and nominated by princes; any province could stay out and form its own union; defence to remain with Britain during the war.
  • Result: rejected by both the Congress (no real power now; the opt-out clause threatened unity) and the League (no clear Pakistan). Gandhi called it a 'post-dated cheque' (on a crashing bank).

3. The Quit India Movement (1942)

Gandhi with Jawaharlal Nehru at an All India Congress Committee meeting in Bombay. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, free licence)
PointDetails
ResolutionCongress Working Committee at Wardha (14 July 1942); the AICC at Gowalia Tank, Bombay passed the Quit India resolution on 8 August 1942; Gandhi's call: 'Do or Die' ('Karo ya Maro').
CourseEarly on 9 August 1942 ('Operation Zero Hour') all top leaders were arrested; Aruna Asaf Ali hoisted the flag at Gowalia Tank. Leaderless, people attacked railways, telegraph lines and police stations; underground leaders: Jayaprakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia, Aruna Asaf Ali; Usha Mehta ran a secret Congress Radio.
Parallel governmentsBallia (UP, August 1942; Chittu Pandey); Tamluk (Midnapore, Bengal; Jatiya Sarkar, 1942–44; Matangini Hazra was shot leading a procession); Satara (Maharashtra; Prati Sarkar, 1943–45; Nana Patil).
Who stayed awayThe Muslim League, the Communist Party (after the USSR joined the Allies, it called it a 'People's War'), the Hindu Mahasabha, and many princes; the Depressed Classes leadership did not join.
AfterCrushed by 1943; Gandhi's 21-day fast (February 1943) in the Aga Khan Palace; Kasturba died there (February 1944); Gandhi released (May 1944); the C. Rajagopalachari formula (1944) and Gandhi–Jinnah talks (September 1944) failed. Significance: showed India's determination; independence became certain after the war.
AndhraStrikes and protests spread across the Andhra districts; crowds attacked the railway station at Tenali (August 1942), where police firing killed several people; incidents also at Bhimavaram, Chirala and other towns; students and women joined.

4. Subhas Chandra Bose and the INA

Subhas Chandra Bose arriving at Dum Dum aerodrome, Calcutta. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, free licence)
  • Bose's path: Congress president at Haripura (1938) and Tripuri (1939); differences with Gandhi made him resign and form the Forward Bloc (1939); escaped house arrest in Calcutta (January 1941) to Germany (Free India Centre, Azad Hind Radio, an Indian Legion; called 'Netaji'); reached Singapore (July 1943) by submarine.
  • INA: first formed by Captain Mohan Singh (1942) from Indian prisoners of war after the fall of Singapore; Rash Behari Bose led the Indian Independence League; Netaji took command (1943) and proclaimed the Provisional Government of Free India (Azad Hind) at Singapore on 21 October 1943; slogans 'Delhi Chalo', 'Give me blood, and I will give you freedom', 'Jai Hind'; brigades named Gandhi, Nehru, Azad; the women's Rani of Jhansi Regiment (Lakshmi Swaminathan / Sahgal); Netaji hoisted the flag at Port Blair (30 December 1943); INA flag at Moirang, Manipur (April 1944); the Imphal–Kohima campaign failed (1944); Netaji reportedly died in an air crash at Taipei (18 August 1945).
  • INA trials (Red Fort, November 1945): officers Shah Nawaz Khan, Prem Sahgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon (a Muslim, a Hindu and a Sikh) were defended by Bhulabhai Desai, Tej Bahadur Sapru, Jawaharlal Nehru and Asaf Ali; the whole country protested and the sentences were remitted. Royal Indian Navy mutiny (Bombay, 18 February 1946, HMIS Talwar): showed that Britain could no longer trust Indian forces.

5. 1945–46: Simla, Cabinet Mission, Interim Government

EventFacts
Wavell Plan and Simla Conference (June 1945)An all-Indian Executive Council (except the Viceroy and the C-in-C); failed because Jinnah insisted the League alone would nominate all Muslim members.
Elections (1945–46)The Congress won most general seats; the League won most Muslim seats, strengthening its claim to Pakistan. In Britain, Labour (Attlee) had come to power (July 1945).
Cabinet Mission (March–June 1946)Pethick-Lawrence, Stafford Cripps, A.V. Alexander; plan of 16 May 1946: rejected a separate Pakistan; a weak Union (defence, foreign affairs, communications); provinces in three groups: A (Hindu-majority provinces), B (Punjab, NWFP, Sindh), C (Bengal, Assam); a Constituent Assembly of 389 (292 from provinces, 4 from chief commissioners' provinces, 93 from princely States), elected indirectly by provincial assemblies; an interim government.
Direct Action and afterThe League first accepted, then withdrew and called 'Direct Action Day' (16 August 1946): terrible riots in Calcutta, then Noakhali and Bihar. Interim Government formed 2 September 1946 with Nehru as Vice-President of the Executive Council; the League joined in October 1946 (Liaquat Ali Khan, Finance) but obstructed. The Constituent Assembly met on 9 December 1946 (League boycotted). Attlee's statement (20 February 1947): Britain would leave by June 1948.

POLITY · Political parties and the party system; recognition of parties

Understand it first

What is it? A political party is an organised group of people with shared ideas who seek to win elections and form a government. Parties contest elections, form governments or act as the opposition, make policies, shape public opinion and link people with the government. The party system describes how many parties effectively compete: one-party (China), two-party (USA, broadly UK) or multi-party (India). India's Constitution originally did not mention parties; their legal base comes from the freedom to form associations (Art 19(1)(c)), the Representation of the People Act, the Election Commission's Symbols Order and the Tenth Schedule.

1. India's party system: four phases

PhaseFeatures
1952–1967One-party dominance: the Congress won at the Centre and in almost all States; Rajni Kothari called it the 'Congress system' (opposition worked as pressure groups inside and outside the party).
1967–19891967: the Congress lost power in several States (coalitions, 'Aaya Ram Gaya Ram' defections); 1977: the Janata Party formed the first non-Congress government at the Centre after the Emergency; Congress dominance returned in 1980 and 1984.
1989–2014Coalition era: no single party won a Lok Sabha majority; regional parties became powerful at the Centre (National Front, United Front, NDA, UPA).
2014 onwardsIn 2014, a single party (the BJP) won a Lok Sabha majority for the first time in 30 years, governing with allies; regional parties remain strong in many States.

2. Registration and recognition

  • Registration: under Section 29A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, a party registers with the Election Commission, pledging loyalty to the Constitution, socialism, secularism and democracy and the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India. Most registered parties remain 'registered unrecognised'.
  • Recognition: the EC recognises National and State parties under the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968, based on votes and seats in the last general election (criteria in the diagram). The EC reviews status after elections; for example, in 2023 it listed six national parties: BJP, INC, BSP, CPI(M), AAP and NPP (the National People's Party).

IssueDetails
FundingParties must report donations above ₹20,000 (Sec 29C); the Electoral Bonds Scheme (2018) allowed anonymous donations, but the Supreme Court struck it down on 15 February 2024 (ADR case) as violating voters' right to information; the Indrajit Gupta Committee (1998) suggested partial State funding of elections.

SCIENCE · Nuclear technology: reactors, power plants, radioisotopes

Understand it first

What is it? The nucleus of an atom holds enormous energy. In fission, a heavy nucleus (like uranium-235) is hit by a neutron and splits into two smaller nuclei, releasing heat and 2–3 more neutrons, which split more nuclei: a chain reaction. A nuclear reactor keeps this chain reaction steady and controlled; the heat makes steam, which turns a turbine to produce electricity, with no carbon dioxide. (In fusion, light nuclei such as hydrogen join, as in the Sun; it is still experimental, e.g. ITER, where India is a partner.) Nuclear science also gives radioisotopes for medicine, farming and industry.

Parts of a nuclear reactor

1. Reactor types and the three-stage programme

  • Types: PHWR (pressurised heavy water reactor): natural uranium fuel with heavy water as moderator and coolant; India's mainstay. LWR/PWR: enriched uranium with ordinary water (e.g. the Russian VVER at Kudankulam). Fast breeder reactor (FBR): no moderator, liquid sodium coolant; produces more fissile fuel than it uses. AHWR: a proposed thorium-based design.
  • Why three stages? India has little uranium but some of the world's largest thorium reserves (in monazite beach sands of Kerala, Odisha, Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh). Thorium is not fissile; it must first be turned into U-233 in a reactor. So Homi J. Bhabha planned three stages (1954). The 500 MW Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor (PFBR), Kalpakkam began fuel loading in 2024.
Three-stage programme

2. Institutions, plants and policy

TopicFacts
InstitutionsAtomic Energy Commission (1948), chair Homi Bhabha; Department of Atomic Energy (1954), under the PM; BARC (Trombay, Mumbai); IGCAR (Kalpakkam); NPCIL (1987) runs power plants; BHAVINI (2003) for fast breeders; AERB (1983) is the safety regulator; Uranium Corporation of India (mines at Jaduguda, Jharkhand, and Tummalapalle, Kadapa (YSR) district, AP); Nuclear Fuel Complex, Hyderabad; IREL processes beach-sand minerals.
PlantsApsara (1956), Trombay: Asia's first research reactor; Tarapur (1969): first nuclear power station; others at Rawatbhatta, Kalpakkam, Narora, Kakrapar (the first indigenous 700 MW PHWR, 2021), Kaiga and Kudankulam (largest, Russian VVERs); Kovvada (Srikakulam, AP) is a planned site. Installed nuclear capacity is about 8,000+ MW (about 3% of electricity); the target is 100 GW by 2047 with small modular reactors (Budget 2025).
Tests, doctrine, agreementsPokhran-I (18 May 1974, 'Smiling Buddha'); Pokhran-II (11 and 13 May 1998, 'Operation Shakti'): 11 May is National Technology Day; doctrine of 'No First Use' and credible minimum deterrence (2003); India has not signed the NPT or the CTBT; India–US civil nuclear deal (2008) with an NSG waiver; Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act, 2010.

3. Radioisotopes and safety

UseExamples
MedicineCobalt-60 for cancer radiotherapy; Iodine-131 to treat thyroid disorders and cancer; Technetium-99m for imaging scans; sterilising syringes and surgical items.
Agriculture and foodRadiation-induced mutation gives new crop varieties (BARC's groundnut, pulses); food irradiation stops sprouting and extends shelf life (onions, potatoes, mangoes); sterile insect technique for pest control.
Industry and researchRadiography to find cracks in welds and pipes; thickness gauges; carbon-14 dating of ancient objects.
Safety concernsAccidents at Chernobyl (1986) and Fukushima (2011); long-lived radioactive waste; high cost and local opposition (e.g. Kudankulam protests).
