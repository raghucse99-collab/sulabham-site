GEOGRAPHY · Agriculture and agro-based activities in India and Andhra Pradesh

Understand it first

What is it? Agriculture is growing crops and rearing animals for food, fibre and income. It still employs about 45% of India's workers but gives only about 16–18% of national output (GVA), so incomes per farmer are low. What grows where depends on soil, rainfall, temperature and irrigation (Days 32–36). Agro-based activities (dairy, poultry, fisheries, sericulture, food processing, sugar, textiles) add value and jobs. Learn it as importance → seasons → major crops and where → revolutions → problems → policy and schemes → allied activities → Andhra Pradesh.

2. Revolutions, problems and policy

Agricultural revolutions
  • World ranks: India is first in milk, pulses, jute and millets, and second in rice, wheat, sugarcane, fruits and vegetables, cotton (among the top two), and a leading fish producer. Problems: small and fragmented holdings (average about 1.1 hectares; over 85% small and marginal farmers), dependence on monsoon (only about half of net sown area is irrigated), low productivity, post-harvest losses, weak markets and middlemen, debt, soil and water depletion, climate risk.
  • Policy: Minimum Support Price (MSP) for 22 crops (plus FRP for sugarcane), recommended by the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP), at least 1.5 times the cost of production (A2+FL) since 2018. PM-KISAN (2019): ₹6,000 a year in three instalments to land-holding farmers; PM Fasal Bima Yojana (2016) (crop insurance; farmer premium 2% kharif, 1.5% rabi, 5% commercial/horticulture); Kisan Credit Card (1998); e-NAM (2016) online mandis; Soil Health Card (2015); PM Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (2015; 'Per Drop More Crop'); 10,000 FPOs; Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (₹1 lakh crore); Digital Agriculture Mission (2024; AgriStack, farmer IDs); PM Dhan-Dhaanya Krishi Yojana (2025) for 100 low-yield districts; Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses; National Mission on Natural Farming (2024).
  • Agro-based activities: dairy (Operation Flood; Amul model co-operatives; Rashtriya Gokul Mission for native breeds); poultry (AP, TN, Telangana lead in eggs); fisheries (PM Matsya Sampada Yojana, 2020; India is the second-largest fish producer; shrimp is a top seafood export); sericulture (India second in silk; Karnataka first; AP for tussar and mulberry); food processing (PMKSY 'Sampada', mega food parks, PLI for food processing); agro-industries: sugar, cotton textiles, jute mills, vegetable oils, rice mills.

3. Andhra Pradesh

AP agriculture: crops and regions
  • Where AP leads: aquaculture (AP is India's top State for shrimp and farmed fish; about a third of India's fish output), chillies (Guntur's market is among Asia's largest; the Spices Board link), oil palm (largest area, Eluru–West Godavari), Virginia (FCV) tobacco (Prakasam; Tobacco Board HQ in Guntur), eggs, papaya, tomato, sweet orange, mango, banana, cocoa and other horticulture. Agriculture and allied sectors give AP a larger share of its GSDP than the national average (about a third). GI products: Banganapalle mango, Guntur Sannam chilli, Araku Valley coffee, Bandar laddu, Tirupati laddu, Atreyapuram pootharekulu.
  • State support: village-level Rythu Seva Kendras (earlier Rythu Bharosa Kendras) for seeds, advice and procurement; e-Crop booking for every field; Annadata Sukhibhava (2025) income support of ₹20,000 a year per farmer family including the Centre's PM-KISAN ₹6,000 (check the current amount); APCNF natural farming; free crop insurance at times; micro-irrigation (AP is a leader in drip). Agro-industries: sugar mills (Godavari, Krishna, Chittoor), rice mills, aqua processing and export (Visakhapatnam, Kakinada, Nellore), cotton ginning (Guntur, Kurnool), Vijaya dairy, cashew processing (Palasa), coir (Konaseema), food parks.
Other angles asked in exams

Other angles asked: (1) Swaminathan Commission (National Commission on Farmers, 2004–06) suggested MSP at cost + 50% (C2). (2) Cropping intensity = gross cropped area ÷ net sown area × 100. (3) Shifting cultivation has local names: jhum (NE), podu (AP, Telangana), kumari (Western Ghats). (4) Doubling Farmers' Income committee: Ashok Dalwai. (5) Land reforms are a State subject; Operation Barga (West Bengal) is a classic example. (6) Agri exports: rice, marine products and spices lead; Basmati has a GI.

ECONOMY · The recent Union Budget (2026–27)

Understand it first

What is it? The Union Budget is the Government's plan of receipts and spending for one financial year (1 April to 31 March). The Constitution calls it the 'Annual Financial Statement' (Article 112); the word 'budget' is not used. Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman presented the 2026–27 Budget on 1 February 2026 (her ninth in a row), the first under the 16th Finance Commission period and the first with the new Income-tax Act in force. Learn it as how a Budget is passed → the 2026–27 framework → size, deficit and debt → where money comes from and goes → tax changes → new missions by sector → what it means for AP.

1. How a Budget becomes law

Stage / termMeaning
Presentation and discussionPresented in the Lok Sabha on 1 February (since 2017; the Railway Budget was merged with it the same year); general discussion, then Departmental Standing Committees scrutinise Demands for Grants (Art 113; voted only by the Lok Sabha).
Cut motionsPolicy cut (reduce to ₹1: disapproval of policy), economy cut (reduce by a set amount), token cut (reduce by ₹100: a specific grievance).
Appropriation and Finance BillsThe Appropriation Bill (Art 114) authorises withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund of India (Art 266); the Finance Bill gives effect to tax proposals. Both are Money Bills (Art 110). Vote on account (Art 116) lets the Government spend for a few months (used in an election year with an 'interim budget'). The Contingency Fund (Art 267) meets urgent unforeseen costs; charged expenditure (President's pay, judges' salaries, CAG, debt charges) is not voted.

2. Budget 2026–27: the numbers

Budget 2026–27 at a glance
  • Framework: built around three 'kartavyas' (duties): (1) accelerate and sustain growth, (2) build human capacity, (3) inclusive development, towards Viksit Bharat 2047. Size: total expenditure about ₹53.5 lakh crore; capital expenditure ₹12.2 lakh crore (up from about ₹11.2 lakh crore). Fiscal deficit 4.3% of GDP (₹16.96 lakh crore; 2025–26 revised 4.4%); revenue deficit 1.5%; Central debt about 55.6% of GDP, on a path to 50 ± 1% by 2030–31; assumes 10% nominal GDP growth. Devolution: the States' share in the divisible pool is kept at 41%, accepting the 16th Finance Commission (Arvind Panagariya).
  • Receipts (estimates): gross tax about ₹44 lakh crore: income tax ~₹14.7 lakh crore (now the largest single tax), corporation tax ~₹12.3 lakh crore, GST ~₹10.2 lakh crore, excise ~₹3.9 lakh crore, customs ~₹2.7 lakh crore; tax devolution to States about ₹15.3 lakh crore; RBI and public-sector bank dividends about ₹3.2 lakh crore; gross market borrowing about ₹17.2 lakh crore. Big spending heads: interest payments (the largest), defence ~₹5.95 lakh crore, food subsidy ~₹2.3 lakh crore (free grain under PMGKAY), fertiliser subsidy ~₹1.7 lakh crore, pensions ~₹3 lakh crore.
  • Tax changes: the Income-tax Act, 2025 takes effect from April 2026 with simpler forms; no change in slabs (nil tax up to ₹12 lakh continues); Minimum Alternate Tax cut from 15% to 14%; share buybacks taxed as capital gains; STT on futures and options raised; TCS on overseas tours and education/medical remittances cut to 2%; a tax holiday till 2047 for foreign cloud firms using Indian data centres; a one-time foreign-asset disclosure window; customs: 17 cancer drugs exempted, duty on personal imports cut to 10%, lower duty on some critical minerals.

Other angles asked in exams

Other angles asked: (1) Outcome Budget links money to results; Gender Budget statement since 2005–06. (2) The Economic Survey (by the Chief Economic Adviser) is released a day before the Budget. (3) Interim Budget (election year) vs full Budget; vote on account is Art 116.
