GEOGRAPHY · Transport and communication

Understand it first

What is it? Transport moves people and goods from where they are to where they are needed; communication moves messages and information. Together they are the 'lifelines' of the economy: a farmer's chillies reach a Guntur market by road, then a port by rail, then another country by ship. Each mode has a strength: roads reach every village (door-to-door, short distance), railways carry bulk goods and crowds over long distances cheaply, waterways are the cheapest for heavy cargo, pipelines carry oil and gas without stopping, and air is the fastest but costliest. A good network lowers logistics cost (India's is high, around 13–14% of GDP by older estimates; the 2023 NCAER study put it near 8–9%). Learn it as roads → railways → waterways and ports → air and pipelines → communication → Andhra Pradesh.

Five modes of transport: strengths

1. Roads

  • Network: India has the second-largest road network in the world (about 63 lakh km, after the USA). National Highways (NH) are only about 2% of road length but carry about 40% of traffic. NH length grew from about 91,287 km (2014) to about 1,46,572 km (December 2025); about 5,364 km of high-speed (access-controlled) corridors are open. NHs are built by the Union through NHAI (1988 Act, set up 1995), NHIDCL and the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways; State Highways by States (PWD); district and rural roads by local bodies and States.
  • Big projects: Golden Quadrilateral (about 5,846 km; links Delhi–Mumbai–Chennai–Kolkata; NHDP 1998–99, under PM Vajpayee); North–South corridor (Srinagar–Kanyakumari) and East–West corridor (Silchar–Porbandar) meet at Jhansi; Bharatmala Pariyojana (2017): economic corridors, border and coastal roads, expressways; PM Gram Sadak Yojana (PMGSY, 25 December 2000) for all-weather roads to villages; Setu Bharatam (rail over-bridges). Longest NH: NH-44 (Srinagar–Kanyakumari, about 4,100 km). Expressways: Delhi–Mumbai Expressway, Mumbai–Pune (first, 2002), Purvanchal, Samruddhi.
  • Odd-even numbering of NHs (2010 system): north–south NHs carry even numbers that rise from east to west; east–west NHs carry odd numbers that rise from north to south. So NH-44 runs north–south and NH-16 (the east-coast highway through Srikakulam–Visakhapatnam–Vijayawada–Nellore to Chennai) runs along the east. Border Roads Organisation (BRO, 1960) builds roads in border areas: Atal Tunnel (Rohtang, 2020), the Umling La road (among the world's highest motorable roads).

2. Railways

  • History: first passenger train Bombay (Bori Bunder)–Thane, 16 April 1853 (34 km; Lord Dalhousie); in the south, Royapuram–Wallajah Road (1856). Nationalised in stages; Railway Board 1905. The Railway Budget was separate from 1924 (Acworth Committee 1920–21) and merged with the Union Budget in 2017.
  • Size: about 69,439 route km (March 2025); fourth-largest network (after the USA, China, Russia) and among the largest employers. Electrification about 99%+ of broad gauge (99.6% in 2025–26); about 741 crore passengers carried in 2025–26. Gauges: broad (1.676 m), metre (1 m), narrow (0.762/0.610 m); Project Unigauge (1992) converts to broad gauge. Hill railways Darjeeling (1999), Nilgiri (2005), Kalka–Shimla (2008) are UNESCO World Heritage (together the 'Mountain Railways of India').
  • Zones: 18 zones now: the South Coast Railway (SCoR), headquarters Visakhapatnam, began working on 1 June 2026 (Waltair division reorganised; Indian Railways' logo now has 18 stars). Others to remember: Northern (New Delhi), South Central (Secunderabad), East Coast (Bhubaneswar), Southern (Chennai), Konkan Railway (a separate corporation, 1998, 741 km along the west coast).
  • Modern projects: Dedicated Freight Corridors: Eastern DFC (Ludhiana–Sonnagar, ~1,337 km) and Western DFC (Dadri–JNPT, ~1,506 km); about 2,741 km commissioned by October 2025. Vande Bharat semi-high-speed trains (2019, made at ICF Chennai); Mumbai–Ahmedabad High-Speed Rail ('bullet train') with Japan (~508 km); Kavach (home-made anti-collision system); Amrit Bharat Station Scheme; the Chenab bridge (world's highest rail arch bridge) and Anji Khad bridge on the USBRL line to Kashmir (2025); Pamban vertical-lift sea bridge (2025). Factories: CLW Chittaranjan (electric locos), BLW Varanasi, ICF Chennai, RCF Kapurthala, Rail Wheel Factory Yelahanka.

4. Communication and Andhra Pradesh

Communication in India: milestones
  • Personal communication: India Post (unified 1854, Dalhousie; the world's largest postal network, about 1.6 lakh post offices, most in villages); PIN code (6 digits) introduced 15 August 1972 (first digit = region; 9 = Army Post Office); Speed Post 1986; India Post Payments Bank (2018). Telegraph service closed 15 July 2013. Telephone: about 120 crore subscribers, mostly mobile; teledensity ~85%; 5G launched 1 October 2022; BharatNet takes optical fibre to gram panchayats; Telecommunications Act 2023 replaced the Indian Telegraph Act 1885; regulator TRAI (1997); dispute tribunal TDSAT.
  • Mass communication: radio broadcasting began 1927 (Bombay); Indian State Broadcasting Service 1930 → All India Radio 1936 → 'Akashvani' 1957; Doordarshan: first TV 15 September 1959 (Delhi), colour TV and national broadcast at the 1982 Asian Games; Prasar Bharati (1997) runs AIR and DD. Satellites INSAT/GSAT made nationwide TV and phones possible. Press: Registrar of Newspapers for India (RNI), now under the Press and Registration of Periodicals Act 2023; Press Council of India (1966/1978). Films certified by CBFC.
  • Andhra Pradesh: NH-16 runs along the whole coast (Ichchapuram to Tada), with links like NH-44 (Anantapur–Kurnool) and NH-40 (Kurnool–Kadapa–Chittoor); Bengaluru–Chennai and Raipur–Visakhapatnam expressways cross the State. Rail: the new South Coast Railway (Visakhapatnam); junctions Vijayawada (among the busiest), Guntakal, Renigunta, Gudur; the Kottavalasa–Kirandul (KK) line carries Bailadila iron ore to Vizag through the Araku valley and Borra tunnels. Ports: Visakhapatnam (major), Gangavaram, Krishnapatnam, Kakinada (deep-water and anchorage), and new Ramayapatnam, Machilipatnam, Mulapeta ports. Airports: Visakhapatnam, Vijayawada (Gannavaram), Tirupati (Renigunta), Rajahmundry, Kadapa, Kurnool (Orvakal), Puttaparthi; the new Alluri Sitarama Raju International Airport, Bhogapuram (Vizianagaram district) opened in 2026. NW-4 (Krishna–Godavari, Buckingham Canal) is in AP. Vijayawada and Vizag metro rail projects are planned.

Other angles asked: (1) PM Gati Shakti (October 2021): a GIS master plan joining 16 ministries for infrastructure planning; National Logistics Policy (17 September 2022); LEADS index ranks States on logistics; Logistics Performance Index (World Bank): India 38th (2023). (2) Multimodal logistics parks and Inland Container Depots; FASTag (mandatory 2021). (3) The Golden Quadrilateral joins the four metros; the NS–EW corridors cross at Jhansi. (4) Kandla was built after Partition to replace Karachi; Kolkata is a riverine port that needs constant dredging; Kochi is a natural harbour on a lagoon; Mormugao exports iron ore. (5) Sea routes: India's trade passes the Strait of Malacca, Bab-el-Mandeb, Hormuz; the Suez route links Europe. (6) Ro-Ro ferries, seaplanes, ropeways (Parvatmala, 2022). (7) Vande Bharat sleeper and Namo Bharat (RRTS, Delhi–Meerut) are new categories.

ECONOMY · Indian agriculture: cropping pattern, production and productivity

Understand it first

What is it? Three words that exams mix up. Cropping pattern = which crops are grown, and in what share of the land, in a place at a time (for example, how much of the cropped area is under rice, wheat, pulses, cotton). Production = the total quantity harvested (million tonnes). Productivity (yield) = output per hectare (tonnes or kg per hectare). Production can rise because area rises or because yield rises; India's growth since the Green Revolution came mainly from yield. Agriculture still employs about 45% of workers but gives only about 16–18% of GVA, so raising productivity is the key to raising farm incomes. Learn it as land use → cropping pattern and its causes → production record → productivity and why it is low → policies → Andhra Pradesh.

1. Land use and cropping pattern

From land to cropped area
  • Land use (nine-fold classification): of India's reporting area (about 30.5 crore ha), the net sown area is about 14 crore ha (about 46% of reporting area, about 43% of geographical area); forests about 22–23% (recorded); the rest is barren, non-agricultural use, pastures, fallows, etc. Gross cropped area (GCA) counts land as many times as it is sown in a year: about 21.9 crore ha. Cropping intensity = GCA ÷ NSA × 100 ≈ 155% (higher where irrigation allows two or three crops, e.g. Punjab ~190%). About 55% of net sown area is irrigated; the rest is rain-fed.
  • India's cropping pattern: foodgrains still take the largest share of GCA (about 60%, down from about 75% in 1950–51). Rice has the largest area (about 4.7 crore ha), then wheat (about 3.1 crore ha). The share of commercial/cash crops (cotton, sugarcane, oilseeds) and of horticulture has risen; coarse cereals (millets) lost area to rice and wheat after the Green Revolution. Horticulture production (~36 crore t) now exceeds foodgrains (since about 2012–13).
  • What decides the cropping pattern? Geographical: soil, rainfall, temperature, irrigation (canals and tube-wells made Punjab–Haryana rice–wheat). Economic: prices and MSP, assured procurement (mainly rice and wheat), input costs, market access, farm size. Institutional and technological: HYV seeds, credit, land tenure, research, government schemes. Cropping systems: mono-cropping, multiple cropping (two or more crops a year), mixed cropping (two crops together in one field, as insurance), intercropping (in rows), relay cropping, crop rotation (e.g. a legume after a cereal to restore nitrogen).

Other angles asked in exams

Other angles asked: (1) Cropping intensity formula (GCA ÷ NSA × 100) and why irrigation raises it. (2) Production = area × yield: a question may give two of the three. (3) Terms of trade between agriculture and industry; farm income: NSO's survey (2018–19) put an agricultural household's average monthly income at about ₹10,218. (4) 'Doubling Farmers' Income' committee (Ashok Dalwai, 2016–18). (5) Food security vs nutrition security: the shift from cereals to protein, fruit, vegetables, milk changes the cropping pattern. (6) Paddy in Punjab–Haryana causes groundwater depletion and stubble burning; schemes pay farmers to diversify (crop diversification programme). (7) International Year of Millets 2023; 'Shree Anna'. (8) Agriculture is a State subject (List II entry 14), though the Union runs most schemes.
