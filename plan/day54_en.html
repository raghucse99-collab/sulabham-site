INDIAN SOCIETY · Constitutional and statutory provisions for SCs, STs, minorities, BCs, women, persons with disabilities and children

Understand it first

What is it? Equality in law (Art 14) is not enough when groups start from very unequal positions. So the Constitution allows 'protective discrimination': special provisions for weaker groups (Art 15(3), 15(4), 16(4), 46) without breaking equality. Constitutional provisions are written into the Constitution itself (rights, reservations, commissions). Statutory provisions are laws made by Parliament or State legislatures (e.g. the SC/ST Prevention of Atrocities Act). This topic pulls together what we studied on Days 47–51 group by group, adds the commissions, key Amendments and court cases, and ends with Andhra Pradesh's reservation structure. It is one of the most-asked areas in Group 1 and Group 2.

3. Andhra Pradesh and evaluation

Reservation structure in Andhra Pradesh State jobs
  • AP's structure: SC 15%, ST 6%, BC 29% (groups A 7%, B 10%, C 1%, D 7%, E 4%), EWS 10%; horizontal reservation for women (33⅓%) and persons with disabilities (4%); 50% of seats in local bodies for women. After *Davinder Singh (2024)*, AP sub-classified SCs in 2025 on the Rajiv Ranjan Mishra Commission report: Group I (Relli and related castes) 1%, Group II (Madiga and related) 6.5%, Group III (Mala and related) 7.5%. AP has its own State Commissions for SCs, STs, BCs, women, minorities and child rights.
  • Vertical vs horizontal reservation: vertical quotas (SC, ST, BC, EWS) are separate blocks; horizontal quotas (women, PwD, ex-servicemen) cut across them and are filled within each vertical block (*Indra Sawhney*; *Saurav Yadav, 2020*). Reservation in promotion: *M. Nagaraj (2006)* needed data on backwardness, adequacy and efficiency; *Jarnail Singh (2018)* dropped the need to prove SC/ST backwardness but applied creamy layer in promotions.
  • Evaluation: these provisions have created a new middle class among SCs, STs and BCs and raised political representation; but benefits concentrate in better-off sub-groups (hence sub-classification, creamy layer), backlog vacancies remain, atrocity conviction rates are low, private-sector jobs have no reservation, and laws for women and the disabled suffer weak enforcement. Way forward: data (caste census in the 2027 Census), sub-classification, education quality, implementation audits and awareness.

AP ECONOMY · Agriculture and allied sectors: production trends and cropping pattern

Understand it first

What is it? Agriculture and allied sectors means crops, horticulture (fruits, vegetables, spices, plantations), livestock (milk, eggs, meat), fisheries and aquaculture and forestry. In Andhra Pradesh they give about 35% of GVA (current prices), far above India's ~18%, and support most families. Production trends show how output changes over time; the cropping pattern is which crops are grown, where and in which season, and how this shifts (e.g. from paddy to fish ponds, or from groundnut to horticulture). Today we learn the size and growth of the sector, land and water, seasons and regions, crops, horticulture, livestock and fisheries, the changing pattern, problems and policy.

1. Size, growth, land and water

Sub-sectors of AP's agriculture and allied GVA
  • Size and growth (Socio-Economic Survey 2024–25): agriculture and allied GVA ₹2,32,296 crore at constant prices, growth 10.7%; fisheries (₹77,085 crore) is now the largest sub-sector, followed by livestock and horticulture; crops alone are only about 11% of the sector. In Q1 2026–27 the sector grew about 14%, led by fisheries (~24%) and livestock. Crops are no longer the main engine; allied sectors are.
  • Land: net area sown ~49.6 lakh ha (about 30% of the geographical area; it has fallen from ~67 lakh ha in 2008–09 as land goes to fallows, towns, fish ponds and other uses); cropping intensity ~1.21; average holding 0.86 ha (2021–22), down from 0.94 ha; about 97 lakh holdings, mostly marginal and small. Water: net irrigated ~26 lakh ha; sources: canals (Godavari, Krishna, Pennar deltas; Nagarjuna Sagar, Somasila), tanks (north coastal and Rayalaseema), groundwater (bore wells); AP is a leader in micro-irrigation (~38 lakh acres). Rainfall: SW monsoon (June–Sept, normal ~575 mm) and NE monsoon (Oct–Dec, normal ~285 mm), so south coastal AP and Rayalaseema depend more on the NE monsoon.
  • Foodgrains (2024–25, 2nd advance estimates): 161.9 lakh tonnes (+12.9%) from 37.5 lakh ha: kharif 88.1, rabi 73.8 lakh tonnes; paddy 127.2 lakh tonnes (kharif 78.8, rabi 48.4) from about 21 lakh ha. AP is among India's top rice producers and supplies rice to other States through the central pool. Pulses: Bengal gram, red gram, black gram and green gram (rice-fallow pulses in the deltas).

3. Changing pattern, problems and policy

  • Shifts in the cropping pattern: (1) paddy fields → fish and shrimp ponds in the deltas (higher income, but salinity and pollution); (2) groundnut → horticulture (mango, sweet orange, tomato, banana) in dry areas with drip irrigation; (3) foodgrains → commercial crops (chillies, cotton, maize, oil palm); (4) millets shrinking but now being revived; (5) rice-fallow pulses in the deltas; (6) oil palm expansion under NMEO-OP. Drivers: prices and markets, irrigation, labour shortage, technology, risk and government schemes.
  • Problems: small holdings and tenant farmers (many without land records, hence weak access to credit and insurance; AP's Crop Cultivator Rights Card Act, 2019); droughts in Rayalaseema and cyclones and floods on the coast (e.g. Michaung 2023, Montha 2025); price crashes (tomato, onion, chillies); falling groundwater; soil and water salinity from aquaculture; high input costs; farmer indebtedness.
  • Policy: Annadata Sukhibhava (₹20,000 a year with PM-KISAN); about 10,700 Rythu Seva Kendras (seeds, fertiliser, advice, e-crop booking); e-Crop digital crop records; crop insurance (about 35 lakh farmers in 2024); APCNF – natural farming (about 9.5 lakh farmers; target 41 lakh by 2028–29); micro-irrigation; Polavaram, Handri–Neeva and other projects; food processing parks; FPOs; Swarna Andhra target of making AP a global food-processing hub.
