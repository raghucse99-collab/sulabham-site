POLITY · International relations: India's foreign policy, neighbours and global bodies

Understand it first

What is it? Foreign policy is how a country deals with other countries to protect its security, economic interests, people abroad and values. It is shaped by geography (long land borders, the Indian Ocean), history (colonialism, Partition), economy (energy imports, trade, technology), domestic politics and the world order. Under the Constitution, foreign affairs, treaties, war and peace are in the Union List (entries 10–21); Art 51 (DPSP) asks the State to promote international peace, respect international law and settle disputes by arbitration; Art 253 lets Parliament make laws to implement treaties, even on State subjects. Today we learn the principles and their evolution, relations with neighbours, major powers, global and regional bodies, and the Andhra Pradesh link.

1. Principles and evolution

Milestones in India's foreign policy
  • Panchsheel (1954): five principles in the India–China agreement on trade with Tibet: mutual respect for territorial integrity and sovereignty, non-aggression, non-interference, equality and mutual benefit, peaceful coexistence. Non-Alignment: staying out of Cold War blocs while keeping freedom of judgment; Bandung (1955) Afro-Asian conference; NAM founded at Belgrade (1961) by Nehru, Nasser (Egypt), Tito (Yugoslavia), Sukarno (Indonesia) and Nkrumah (Ghana).
  • Later shifts: Indo-Soviet Treaty (1971) before the Bangladesh war; nuclear tests 1974 and 1998; nuclear doctrine (2003): no first use, credible minimum deterrence; Look East (1991, P.V. Narasimha Rao) → Act East (2014) with ASEAN and East Asia; Gujral Doctrine (1996): generosity to smaller neighbours without demanding reciprocity; Neighbourhood First (2014); SAGAR (2015): Security and Growth for All in the Region → MAHASAGAR (Mauritius, 2025) for the Global South; today 'strategic autonomy' / multi-alignment: working with the US in the Quad and with Russia and China in BRICS and SCO at the same time.

3. Major powers, global bodies and Andhra Pradesh

The UN Security Council chamber, New York: India seeks a permanent seat (G4). (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, free licence)
  • Major powers: USA: civil nuclear deal (2008), defence partnership, iCET/TRUST technology initiative; tariffs rose to 50% (Aug 2025) and an interim trade deal cut them to about 18% (Feb 2026). Russia: 'special and privileged strategic partner'; S-400, oil imports, Kudankulam. EU: India–EU FTA concluded (Jan 2026). UK: CETA signed (July 2025). Japan: Shinkansen (Mumbai–Ahmedabad), Sri City firms. Gulf: about 90 lakh Indians; remittances ~$135 billion (2024–25, world's highest).
  • Global and regional bodies: UN (1945): India a founding member; elected to the UNSC 8 times (last 2021–22); seeks a permanent seat with the G4 (India, Brazil, Germany, Japan). G20: India's presidency 2023, New Delhi Declaration, African Union admitted. BRICS (2009): expanded in 2024–25 (Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE, Indonesia); India chairs BRICS in 2026. SCO (2001): India a member since 2017. Quad (2007, revived 2017): India, US, Japan, Australia. SAARC (1985, Dhaka): secretariat Kathmandu; no summit since 2014. BIMSTEC (1997): seven Bay of Bengal States, secretariat Dhaka, charter in force 2024. IORA (1997), ISA (2015, HQ Gurugram), CDRI (2019), IMEC (2023), I2U2.
  • Andhra Pradesh link: Visakhapatnam is HQ of the Eastern Naval Command and hosted the International Fleet Review (2016) and IFR and MILAN (Feb 2026); AP's ports and the Act East policy link it to ASEAN; Japanese, Korean and Taiwanese firms at Sri City and Anantapur (Kia); lakhs of AP workers in the Gulf (from the Godavari districts, YSR Kadapa, Annamayya and others), supported by APNRTS (Non-Resident Telugu Society); the Ganga and Teesta issues show why water diplomacy matters to a delta State.
INS Visakhapatnam, a destroyer named after AP's port city: the Navy supports India's Indian Ocean diplomacy. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, free licence)
Other angles asked in exams

Other angles asked: (1) Shimla (1914): McMahon Line; China rejects it. (2) Tashkent (1966) after the 1965 war; Lahore Declaration (1999). (3) Indira Doctrine: South Asia free of outside interference. (4) Chabahar port (Iran; 10-year deal 2024) and INSTC to Russia via Iran. (5) Teesta water dispute with Bangladesh is unresolved. (6) Ministry of External Affairs runs the Indian Foreign Service and Passport Seva; Track II diplomacy uses non-officials. (7) Vaccine Maitri (2021) and Operation Ganga (2022) (Ukraine evacuation), Operation Kaveri (2023) (Sudan). (8) Wassenaar, MTCR (2016) and Australia Group (2018): India is a member; NSG membership is blocked mainly by China.
