HISTORY · South Indian kingdoms: Sangam age to Vijayanagara and the Bahmanis

Understand it first

Why study them together? South India had its own political story: the Mauryas reached only the north of it, and later the Pallavas, Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas and Cholas fought a long three-way contest over the fertile Krishna–Tungabhadra doab and Vengi (coastal Andhra). They created the Dravida and Vesara temple styles, bhakti movements (Alvars and Nayanars), Kannada and Telugu literature, and sea trade to Southeast Asia. Later, Vijayanagara and the Bahmanis fought over the same Raichur doab. Exams ask who built which temple, who defeated whom, inscriptions and authors, and matching of capitals.

Other angles asked in exams

Other angles asked: (1) Kalabhras (c. 3rd–6th century) ended the Sangam age ('dark age' of Tamil history). (2) Kadambas of Banavasi (founder Mayurasharma; Talagunda inscription) and Western Gangas (Gommateshvara at Shravanabelagola, built by minister Chavundaraya). (3) Hoysalas (Belur, Halebidu, star-shaped temples) and Yadavas of Devagiri ended by Delhi Sultanate raids (Malik Kafur, 1310–11). (4) Kakatiyas of Warangal: Rudramadevi, Ramappa temple (UNESCO 2021); Marco Polo visited Motupalli. (5) Pandya revival under Maravarman Kulasekhara (13th c.). (6) Krishnadevaraya also wrote *Jambavati Kalyanam* (Sanskrit). (7) Battle of Talikota was fought near Rakshasi and Tangadi villages. (8) Domingo Paes compared Vijayanagara to Rome.

Exam trap

Sangam: Cheras (Vanji, Muziris), Cholas (Uraiyur, Puhar; Karikala – Kallanai), Pandyas (Madurai, Korkai); Tolkappiyam, Tirukkural, Silappadikaram (Ilango), Manimekalai (Sattanar); tinais; Arikamedu; Satavahanas: Simuka; Gautamiputra (Nasik – Gautami Balasri); Pratishthana, Dhanyakataka; first land grants; Hala – Gathasaptasati; Prakrit; Pallavas (Kanchi): Mahendravarman I (Mattavilasa); Narasimhavarman I Mamalla – Vatapi 642; Rajasimha – Shore Temple, Kailasanatha Kanchi; ghatika; Hiuen Tsang; Alvars, Nayanars; Chalukyas: Pulakeshin II beat Harsha (Narmada), Aihole (Ravikirti 634); Pattadakal (Lokamahadevi, Vesara); Vengi (Kubja Vishnuvardhana 624; Nannaya); Kalyani (Tailapa II 973; Bilhana, Mitakshara); Rashtrakutas: Dantidurga; Manyakheta; Krishna I – Kailasa Ellora; Amoghavarsha – Kavirajamarga, Jain; tripartite struggle; Takkolam 949; Vijayanagara: Harihara, Bukka 1336 (Vidyaranya); Sangama–Saluva–Tuluva–Aravidu; Krishnadevaraya 1509–29, Raichur 1520, Amuktamalyada, Ashtadiggajas; Talikota 1565; Nayankara, Ayagar; Hampi; Lepakshi; Bahmanis: 1347 Gulbarga → Bidar; Mahmud Gawan; five sultanates; Golconda 1518, Charminar 1591, annexed 1687; Gol Gumbaz.
