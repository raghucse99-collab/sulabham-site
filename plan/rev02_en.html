Polity (Days 7–12)

TopicPoints to write
Fundamental Duties and amendmentArt 51A, Part IVA; 42nd Amendment (1976, Swaran Singh Committee), USSR; 10 duties, 11th (education of children 6–14) by the 86th (2002); not enforceable directly. Art 368 (Part XX): bill only in Parliament, no joint sitting, President must assent (24th); simple majority (outside Art 368, e.g. Art 4), special majority, special majority + half the States (federal matters). Key amendments: 1st (Ninth Schedule), 42nd ('mini-Constitution'), 44th (property out of FRs), 61st (voting age 18), 73rd, 74th, 86th, 101st (GST), 103rd (EWS), 104th, 106th (women's reservation).
Basic structureKesavananda Bharati (1973; 13 judges, 7:6); elements: supremacy of the Constitution, secularism, federalism, separation of powers, judicial review, free and fair elections, rule of law, parliamentary democracy, limited amending power; Indira Gandhi v Raj Narain (1975), Minerva Mills (1980), Waman Rao (1981), S.R. Bommai (1994), I.R. Coelho (2007), NJAC case (2015).
Union executivePresident (Arts 52–62): elected by elected MPs and elected MLAs (States, Delhi, Puducherry), single transferable vote; 35 years; impeachment (Art 61) for violating the Constitution, nominated MPs vote; pardon (Art 72), ordinance (Art 123); absolute, suspensive and pocket veto. Vice-President (Art 63), Rajya Sabha chairman. PM and Council of Ministers (Arts 74–75): collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha; size ≤ 15% of the Lok Sabha (91st).
ParliamentLok Sabha: 543 elected (max 550 after the 104th), 5 years, Speaker; Rajya Sabha: max 250 (238 + 12 nominated), permanent, one-third retire every 2 years; Money Bill (Art 110): only in the Lok Sabha, Speaker certifies, Rajya Sabha 14 days; joint sitting (Art 108), Speaker presides, not for Money or Constitution Amendment Bills; Budget (Art 112, Annual Financial Statement), Appropriation (114), vote on account (116); question hour, zero hour, cut motions.
State executive and legislatureGovernor (Arts 153–162): appointed by the President (Art 155), holds office at his pleasure; discretion (Art 163), reservation of Bills (Art 200), ordinances (Art 213); CM and ministers (Art 164), ≤ 15% of the Assembly (AP: 175 seats, so at most 26 ministers including the CM). Legislative Council (Art 169): created or abolished by Parliament on the Assembly's special-majority resolution; six States have councils (AP, Telangana, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, UP); size ≤ one-third of the Assembly, at least 40; members: 1/3 by MLAs, 1/3 local bodies, 1/12 graduates, 1/12 teachers, 1/6 nominated.

Reasoning and Maths (Days 7–12)

  • Statement–argument: a strong argument is important and directly related; 'all countries do it' or emotional points are weak. Statement–conclusion: accept only what must follow from the given facts. Course of action: it must be practical and reduce the problem. Syllogism: draw Venn diagrams; 'some' works both ways, 'all' only one way.
  • Number system: divisibility rules (3, 9: digit sum; 4: last two digits; 8: last three; 11: alternate sum); unit digit cycles (2: 2,4,8,6; 3: 3,9,7,1; 7: 7,9,3,1); HCF × LCM = product of two numbers.
  • Averages: sum = average × count; a new member's value = new sum − old sum. Ratio: combine A:B and B:C by making B equal. Percentage: successive change a + b + ab/100; if price rises r%, cut consumption by r/(100 + r) × 100% to keep spending same.
